# Sales & Closing

> Decisions about sales conversations, objection handling, closing scripts, frame control, qualification, and turning "maybe" into "yes."

**Sources distilled (so far):** $100M Closing Playbook (Hormozi, 2025), ACQ Closer Handbook (Hormozi, 2025 — 170pp, OCR-extracted).

## Frameworks

### The 9-Step Discovery Process — *Source: ACQ Closer Handbook*

Discovery happens after the introduction and before the offer. Goal: 1) extract the prospect's problems in their own words, 2) map those problems to your pre-defined solution categories. The "Pain Cycle" runs steps 3-8 repeatedly until you have enough labeled problems.

| Step | Name | Purpose | Example question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Current** | Establish where they are now | "Why did you reach out? Where are you at?" |
| 2 | **Desired** | Establish where they want to be | "Outside of money, what are you trying to accomplish?" |
| 3 | **Obstacle** | Identify what's blocking them | "What's your biggest roadblock right now? What's holding you back?" |
| 4 | **Reason** | Understand why it's not working | "Why? Can you give me an example?" |
| 5 | **Pull Teeth** | Extract specifics from vague answers | "I see. Can you tell me more? Can you give me an example?" |
| 6 | **Recap** | Restate their problem in their words | "So you're [not getting as many leads as you want]…" |
| 7 | **Label** | Map their problem to YOUR solution categories | "It sounds like you've got some marketing issues…" |
| 8 | **Confirm** | Verify you got it right | "Does that sound about right?" |
| 9 | **Repeat** | Cycle through 3-8 for next problem | "What else is holding you back? What's the next biggest problem?" |

**5 Solution Categories** (every problem must map to one of these — same five Hormozi uses across his portfolio):

| Category | Sample prospect language |
|---|---|
| **Marketing** | "no views/reach," "no ad conversions," "too few leads," "bad quality leads," "personal brand not growing" |
| **Sales** | "can't close," "no one can afford our stuff," "can't find good salespeople," "sales are inconsistent" |
| **Product/Delivery** | "churn is high," "LTV is low," "can't get people to buy again," "can't service customers properly" |
| **People** | "my team sucks," "I have no help," "I'm overwhelmed," "can't find [role]" |
| **Profit** | "not making as much as we want," "cash-flow constrained" |

**The "Pull Teeth" technique:** for vague / confusing / incomplete answers, the response is identical regardless: *"I see. Can you tell me more about that?"* → *"OK cool. Can you give me an example?"* The "give me an example" prompt connects abstract problems to lived experience. That's the goldmine.

**Final Recap (Stacking the Pain):** after multiple Pain Cycles, repeat all the recaps + labels back as one stacked statement. Forces the prospect to feel the cumulative weight of their unaddressed problems.

> *"So to make sure I understand: you tried 3 different marketing agencies, spent $50K, got leads that didn't convert, and now you're hesitant to invest again. So it sounds like the real issue isn't just vendors, ads, and leads — it's that you need an entirely new marketing AND sales process. Does that sound right?"*

### The Plug-and-Chug Offer Stack — *Source: ACQ Closer Handbook*

After Discovery, you have ~2 minutes (~320 words at 150-170 wpm) to present the offer. Don't pitch — *map.*

The 5-step delivery, structured as Problem → Solution → Assure → Benefit → Confirm for each item:

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Transition** — "Based on everything you've said… I think we can help. You good to walk through it?" |
| 2 | **Map (×3)** — for each labeled problem from discovery: |
| 2a | *Problem*: remind them of their problem ("For your 'Not enough leads' issue…") |
| 2b | *Solution*: what specifically you'll do (feature) |
| 2c | *Assure*: why that works / who delivers it (decreases risk) |
| 2d | *Benefit*: the good stuff they get (outcome) |
| 2e | *Confirm*: "Do you think that would help?" |
| 3 | **Stack** — list all 3 solution+benefit pairs together. "So you've got [Solution1] for [Benefit1]… [Solution2] for [Benefit2]… and [Solution3] for [Benefit3]…" |
| 4 | **Ask** — "You ready to [next step]?" |
| 5 | **Drop Price & STFU** — state the price, shut the **** up, wait. |

**Pre-built infrastructure makes this work:** your 5 solution categories don't change. Pre-write the assurance and benefit lines for each. In Discovery you label problems into a category. In Offer you plug-and-chug the pre-written maps. Never wing it.

> **Pro tip:** *"If you ever ask 'Do you have any questions?' you will be benched and made an example of."* Don't invite reasons not to buy.

### The 5 Buying Objection Buckets — *Source: ACQ Closer Handbook*

A million different things people say collapse into 5 categories. Same five categories Hormozi has used across portfolios for a decade. (This taxonomy refines the 3-layer Onion of Blame from the original Closing Playbook by splitting Self into Preference vs Stall.)

| # | Bucket | Solution principle | Sample objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Time** | "Not enough time" never exists — only "not high enough priority." Reframe to priorities. | "I don't have time," "I'm too busy," "Maybe when things slow down" |
| 2 | **Money** | Show how much the *problem* costs vs the solution. Accounts for ~50% of objections. | "I can't afford it," "Too expensive," "Not in my budget" |
| 3 | **Decision-Maker** | 3 sub-steps: get DM on call up front; ask if past approvals predict yes; book follow-up with DM | "I have to talk to my [spouse / partner / boss]," "We make decisions together" |
| 4 | **Preference** | Remind of problem → unpack short/medium/long-term consequences of current path → "change variables = change outcome" | "Not what I had in mind," "It's not for me," "I'd prefer a different approach" |
| 5 | **Stall** | Walk through a decision-making framework + name the cost of waiting (good missed + bad continued) | "Let me sleep on it," "Need time to think," "Can I get back to you?" |

### Looping — the "right way" to handle objections — *Source: ACQ Closer Handbook*

**The wrong way (most salespeople):** catch the prospect in their excuse → call out the bullshit → expect them to buy. This makes them feel stupid → they get defensive → "your closer is an asshole" becomes the new objection. Fantasy land.

**The right way (looping):**

```
1. Acknowledge their objection (no contradiction, no insult)
2. Add to their reasoning (your reframe / counter-context)
3. Loop them back to the offer (re-ask for the sale)
```

**Critical principles:**
- **Never insult the objection itself.** Address content, not the silliness.
- **Tonality matters more than words.** Speed, volume, enunciation, pitch, pauses. With those controlled, the prospect never feels embarrassed.
- **You're not on the debate team.** "If you want to be right, don't get into sales." Maintain agreement; add; loop.
- **The salesperson who asks for the buy the most times closes the most.** The only way to ask many times is to keep the conversation agreeable.
- *"If they truly didn't want what we offered, they'd say so. Most prospects are fishing for permission to buy."*

**The door-to-door BMW story (anti-pattern):** salesperson tells homeowner "you obviously have money, look at your BMW and new deck." Door slams. Manager: *"Even if 'can't afford' is an excuse, you don't challenge them on it. Try: 'That's a fair point. What would need to happen for this to make sense financially?' Same goal, no confrontation."*

### The ACQ Sales Career Path — *Source: ACQ Closer Handbook*

The progression Hormozi uses to staff and develop sales teams. Each level requires demonstrated mastery of the previous before promotion is considered:

| Level | Role | What they prove |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Setter** | Books appointments, runs the Outbound/Inbound Set scripts. *"If you can't set, you can't close."* Everyone starts here, even ex-top-closers from other shops. |
| **2** | **Jr Closer** | Closes from inbound on simple offers. Learns objections. |
| **3** | **Closer** | Closes the full lineup — outbound, complex offers, multi-stakeholder deals. Runs the 9-step Discovery + Plug-and-Chug Offer Stack independently. |
| **4** | **Sr Closer** | Top performer. Mentors new closers. Handles edge cases. |
| **5** | **Closer Manager** | Builds and runs sales teams. Owns hiring, training, accountability. |

**Rule:** *"Qualifying for the next level doesn't mean you automatically jump there. Qualifying means it now makes sense for us to ask if you are undeniably ready for it."* Promotion ≠ assumption.

**Best closers were the best setters.** Same skill, lower stakes. New hires set first to validate fit before risking real opportunities.

**"Garbage man" doctrine** (Jacob's story, also referenced in `leads-and-acquisition.md`): top reps cherry-pick green leads. Garbage-men close the yellow leads everyone discards. Yellows are gold for hungry reps with humility.

### The Three Buckets — *Source: Closing Playbook, "Why Is Closing So Important?"*

Every advertising audience splits into three buckets, not two:

| Bucket | Aka | What they need from sales |
|---|---|---|
| **YES** | Laydowns / qualified | Don't mess up the close. Take payment. |
| **NO** | Unqualified (wrong fit) | Disqualify graciously. Offer downsell or free resource. |
| **MAYBE** | On the fence — value the product but have reservations | **The entire game.** This is who you train for. |

**"The money is in the maybes."** Most businesses sweep maybes into the no bucket. Closing exists to convert as many maybes into genuine yeses (or genuine nos — both are wins).

### Power & Decision Avoidance — *Source: Closing Playbook*

**The thesis:** People on the fence aren't deciding "no" — they're avoiding deciding at all. They lack the *power* to make the decision. Closing isn't manipulation; it's *returning decision-making power* to the prospect.

**Why it matters:**
- "I don't want this" and "I'm avoiding deciding" look identical from outside (no purchase) but are *opposite* internal states.
- Real closing produces real yeses AND real nos. Both are wins. The loss is leaving someone on the avoidance treadmill.
- "The difference between help and manipulation is intention."

---

### The Onion of Blame — *Source: Closing Playbook*

People avoid decisions by blaming things. Blame falls into 3 layers, peeled outside-in:

| Layer | Core argument | Sub-types |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Circumstances** | "I avoid this because of stuff outside my control." | Time, Money, Fit |
| **2. Other People** | "Someone else won't let me / I don't trust you." | Decision Makers (Authority/Permission), Trust/Skepticism |
| **3. Self** | "I'm scared / I might mess this up / I need to think." | Preferences, Rushed Decisions / Avoidance |

**Critical:** prospects often blame multiple layers in one call. When they swap from "it's expensive" to "I need to ask my spouse," that's a sign you're closing correctly — keep peeling.

### STAR Qualification — *Source: Closing Playbook author note*

Before you sell, qualify. A STAR prospect has all four:

| Letter | Means | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Situation | Do they have the problem you solve? |
| **T** | Timing | Do they need it now? |
| **A** | Authority | Do they have the power to decide? |
| **R** | Resources | Do they have the money? |

If S-T-A-R isn't all present, the issue isn't sales — it's an advertising/lead-source problem. (This is the same insight as the leads playbook's "qualify before you spend rep time.")

## Principles (selected from the 28 Rules of Closing)

- **When in doubt, repeat back what they said.** Buys you time + active listening points + diffuses tension. (Don't be a parrot — paraphrase.)
- **Acknowledge or agree, never disagree.** Disagreement triggers hostility. "Totally understand" / "Great point" / "You're right, I forgot to mention…" → then transition into your reframe.
- **Before getting real, get permission.** "Can I be a coach instead of a friend for a second?" / "Can I tell you what I told someone in your exact position?" Then deliver the harder truth.
- **Stack closes from different angles.** Logic close → analogy close → emotional close. Each layer brings them closer.
- **Use nudges when they're on the edge.** A nudge is a one-liner that tips a near-yes into a yes. Different from a full overcome.
- **Never change your price on the spot to get a sale.** Path to bullied-by-customers hell.
- **Don't assume objections mean they won't buy.** Sometimes "It's expensive" = processing out loud. Reply "I agree" and let them keep talking.
- **Only ask for the sale when you think you've got 'em.** If unsure, keep probing. The ask is a closing move, not a discovery move.
- **People want to believe you. They want to buy.** Your job is helping their brain justify the decision they already want to make.
- **Selling happens before the ask. Closing happens after.** Front-load handling. The fewer concerns at "the ask," the cleaner the close.
- **The person who cares the most about the prospect wins the sale.** Keep the prospect — not the sale — as the priority. Comes through in tone.
- **Selling is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust.** You can only build trust if you genuinely want to help.
- **Be kind, not nice.** Nice = afraid to offend. Kind = willing to call them on their BS to genuinely help. Closers ask hard questions because they care.
- **"If you wouldn't say it to a friend, don't say it on a sales call."** And vice versa: if a friend was self-sabotaging, you'd push them.
- **Volume negates luck.** Cross-applies from the leads playbook: many reps × many calls = consistency beats brilliance.

## The Close Library

### All-Purpose closes (the 80/20 — work for almost any objection)

| Close | One-line gist |
|---|---|
| **What's Your Main Concern?** | "What are you afraid of happening?" Forces them to name a real obstacle (not fluff). |
| **The Reason Close** | "The reason you're telling yourself not to is the reason you should." Flips the chain into the lever. |
| **The Hypothetical Close** | "If this were perfect, would you do it?" Then either fix the small detail or expose the real fear. |
| **The Zoom Out Close** | "You want X. We sell X. Could overanalyzing details be the reason you haven't gotten X yet?" |
| **The 1-to-10 Close** | "On a scale of 1-10, where are you? What would make it a 10?" Then handle the gap. |
| **The Best Case / Worst Case Close** | "Best case: you change your life. Worst case (with guarantee): you get free time + free learning. Either way, you win." |
| **The Card Not On Me Close** | "No problem. Open your banking app — your account number is top right. I'll Google your routing number." (Plus: process them via ACH whenever possible.) |

### Time closes — *"I'm too busy"*

- **Better To Start When You're Busy:** "If you can't make it work busy, it won't last. Best time = now."
- **You're Gonna Get Busy Again:** "Wait til you're not busy → get busy again → quit. Learn to do this busy = stick forever."
- **It's Not About Timing, It's About Priorities:** "What are you doing that's more important than [their stated goal]?"
- **The Smartphone Close:** "You spend X hrs/week on social media. You and Bezos have the same 24 hours. The first thing we do is eliminate 90% of what's not working."
- **The When/Then Close:** "When I have time" = "When I'm rich, then I'll save money." Flips order. False premise.

### Money closes — value variant — *"It's too expensive"*

- **It's Good That It's A Lot:** "The fact that this feels significant means you'll take it seriously. If it were $1 you'd ignore it like the others."
- **Good Things Aren't Cheap, Cheap Things Aren't Good:** "How many free PDFs have you downloaded and made no money from?"
- **Would You Even Believe Me If It Were Less?** "Would you believe it could do what I say if it were 1/3 the price?" → They say no → "Then what are we talking about?"
- **It's Not A Lot When You Think About It:** "If this got you $10K/mo, would it be worth it? Then it's not the price — it's whether you believe you can do it."
- **Not What You Make But What It's Worth:** Reframes from sticker to ROI.
- **You're Gonna Spend The Money Either Way:** "In 12 months you'll have spent this much regardless. The only question is whether you spend it on something that matters."
- **You Pay The Price Either Way:** "With years of your life figuring it out, OR with a payment now using time others have already invested."
- **Some Now Or More Later:** "Doing this costs some now; not doing this costs more later." (Inflation of the problem.)
- **Future Favor:** "10 years from now, would you trade this amount to give yourself 10 years of compounding benefit? Of course."

### Money closes — affordability variant — *"I can't afford it"*

- **Resourcefulness Not Resources:** "The difference between a self-made millionaire with nothing and someone with nothing is resourcefulness, not resources." (Phil Knight / Nike example.)
- **Had It Worse And Done Better:** Story-anchored. "Want me to tell you about someone in your exact situation who figured it out?"
- **Everyone Starts At Zero:** "Every self-made millionaire started where you are now. Their resourcefulness compounded into their resources."
- **The Life-Saving Surgery Test:** "If you needed life-saving surgery right now, would you find a way? So the real question is: is saving yourself from the life you're currently living worth it to you?"

### Money closes — competitor variant — *"I can get it cheaper"*

- **Cheap Comparison Close:** "If we were the same price, which would you pick? (Yours.) Why?" → They list reasons you're better → "So why are we talking about price?"
- **Good Fast Cheap:** Pick two. Cheap means trade-off elsewhere.
- **Cheap Or What You Need:** "At end of day, you can buy cheap and not solve the problem, or buy what you need."

### Money closes — haggle variant — *"Can you do it for less?"*

- **The "We Could Do It For More" Close:** "Uhh… we could do it for more (ha)." Or: "$XXX [higher than quoted]." Refuses to enter haggle frame.

### Money closes — fake-objection variant — *"It's not the money…"*

- **It's Not, Not The Money:** "If this were free, would you do it?" If yes → it's not money. Off-ramp to real objection.

### Other People closes — Decision Makers — *"I have to ask my spouse / partner"*

4-step sequence:
1. **"What if they don't give you permission?"**
   - If "I'd do it anyway" → close the sale.
   - If "I wouldn't do it" → off-ramp: "What part wouldn't they approve — price, program, or me?" Handle the real objection.
2. **"Does your spouse approve of your current struggle?"** If they don't → "Then why ask permission to fix something they already don't approve of?"
3. **Role reversal:** "If your spouse needed this for *their* dream, would you support them?"
4. **"Support, not permission":** "It's your life. If you give the deciding power to them and 2 years from now you're still struggling, who do you blame? Resentment sets in. You need their *support*, not their *permission*."

**Last-resort offer:** Three-Day No-Sweat Guarantee. "Sign now, I delay payment 24-72 hrs. If they say stay-poor-fat-stupid-lazy, call me, I tear up the contract. Lock the spot, get time to chat."

### Other People closes — Bad Experience — *"I've been burned before"*

- **Don't Let It Burn You Twice:** "It burned you once when you made the bad investment. Don't let it burn you twice by stopping you from making a good one. Bad eighth-grade boyfriend doesn't mean men aren't for you."

### Self closes — Preferences — *"It's not for me / I want it differently"*

- **Isolate And Solve:** "What specifically would make it for you vs not for you?" Always start here.
- **We Vote With Our Dollars (identity close):** "If you show me what someone spends time and money on, I'll tell you their future. New identity = new priorities." Sephora teen story is the anchor.
- **Pain Of Change < Pain Of Staying The Same:** "We change when staying the same hurts more than changing." Holds a mirror up.
- **You Gotta Change To Change:** "What you've been doing has been getting you what you've been getting."
- **Change The Variables — Change The Outcome:** "If we change this thing you don't like, you'll change the outcome too. I wouldn't feel ethical."
- **The Mechanic Close:** "Asking that question is like asking what's wrong with your car before you bring it in. We need to see it first."
- **The Surgeon Secretary Close:** "Asking me that level of detail is like asking a heart surgeon's secretary to explain what the doctor's gonna do. Beyond my scope."

### Self closes — Rushed Decisions / Avoidance — *"Let me think about it"*

- **What Would Make It A No?** Forces them to crystallize the rejection. Often they can't.
- **Don't Repeat The Same Mistake That Got You Here:** "You're in this situation because you delayed deciding before. Don't make the same decision again."
- **How Much Has Not Making This Decision Cost You?** "The cost of inaction. We never regret what we did; we regret what we didn't do."
- **Another Year Of Almost:** "Another year of *almost* hitting your goals, *almost* hiring the team, *almost* moving into the neighborhood. Tired of *almost*?"
- **It Doesn't Take Time To Make Decisions, It Takes Information:** "You're not gonna go home, sit in a rocking chair, and think 'Hmm…' Three days from now, you'll do laundry, kids' soccer practice, life. The only source of new information is right here."
- **The Gameplan Close:** Three yes-questions: (1) Will this help you get closer to your goals? (2) Do you have access to the money? (3) Can we help you do this better than alone? Three yeses → "What are we waiting for?"
- **This Isn't A Fast Decision:** "You saw the ad. Clicked. Opted in. Set time aside. We've talked for X minutes. You did all this because it's important. Six inches from gold — don't stop digging now."

### Self closes — Guarantee variants

- **Both Options Are Risk-Free:** "Bad news: you can't make an informed decision until you try it. Good news: you can try risk-free. I'm not asking you to decide now, I'm asking you to make an *informed* decision — which requires trying."
- **Decadere:** "Decide is Latin for de-cadere — to cut off. Doing this is a decision. Not doing this is a decision. Which future are you cutting off?"
- **Closer Or Further:** "If you keep doing what you're doing for 5 years, what does life look like? Will this decision get you closer or further from your goal? Then what are we waiting for? We don't need to be snipers — directionally right is enough."

## Heuristics & rules of thumb

- **Selling pre-handles, closing post-handles.** The better your marketing/qualification, the lighter your close work. The worse your marketing, the more thoroughly your sales process has to do.
- **Repeat back + "totally understand" before any close.** Three-second pause + acknowledgment = de-escalation.
- **Stack 2-3 closes per objection, not one.** If close #1 doesn't bite, close #2 reaches from a different angle.
- **Treat "It's expensive" as processing-out-loud, not a no.** Reply: "I agree." Let them keep talking. Often they close themselves.
- **Take their ID first → write up contract → "I'll trade you for the card."** Sequencing avoids the wallet-at-home objection entirely.
- **Take ACH payments.** Lower fees, no expirations, fewer chargebacks, saves sales when card isn't on hand.
- **3-Day No-Sweat Guarantee is a closing tool, not a refund policy.** Mostly required by law anyway. Lets you "lock the spot" while preserving the spouse-conversation-window.
- **Closers are also the best setters** — same skill, different stakes. Hire setters with closer potential.
- **Track three rates per rep:** show rate (work ethic) + close rate (skill) + lead-to-close (real performance). Close rate alone incentivizes cherry-picking.
- **Volume negates luck** — applies inside the call too: stack 3 closes, not 1.

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- **Disagreeing with the prospect.** Triggers hostility. Always acknowledge first, even when you'll reframe immediately.
- **Memorizing every close instead of understanding the reasoning.** You only need 2-3 favorites per objection — but you need to understand *why they work* so you can adapt.
- **Asking for the sale before they're ready.** Premature ask wastes the close. Probe first, ask second.
- **Discounting on the spot to save the deal.** Path to extortion. Use the "We Could Do It For More" close instead.
- **Fixing the prospect's surface objection (price) when the real objection is fear.** Use "If this were perfect/free, would you do it?" to expose the real concern.
- **Selling someone unqualified.** STAR-qualify first. Unqualified prospects aren't a closing problem; they're an advertising problem.
- **Treating one bucket as the whole audience.** Building only for laydowns wastes your maybes. Building for nos wastes ad spend on bad fits.
- **Letting "I need to talk to my spouse" end the call.** It's a power-deflection, not a real obstacle. Run the 4-step sequence.
- **Moralizing or lecturing during the close.** "Be a coach, not a friend" requires permission first. Without it, you sound preachy.
- **Optimizing close-rate alone.** Encourages reps to cherry-pick easy leads and abandon yellows. Track lead-to-close.

## Worked examples / case studies

- **Gym Launch Jan 2017 turning point** *(framing of the entire playbook)*. Hormozi at rock bottom — DUI, business partner stole funds, mother in hospital. Six gym launches scheduled, $6,600 in the hole on day 2. Discovered his sales team had never been trained on objections — "you guys can't handle these? what are you, cashiers?" Wrote what became this playbook in a single weekend. By month-end: broke even. By 12 months: $7M+ in collected revenue (records reproduced in the book). Saved the business.
- **The Ferrari Frame** *(used in objection training)*. "If we were selling Ferraris at this price, people would be clawing over each other. You gotta explain the value. Connect life now to what it could be after they buy."
- **Sephora teen identity story** *(anchor for "Vote With Our Dollars" close)*. Hormozi watching two 13-year-old girls being told they need to budget for makeup as part of becoming women. New identity → new priorities. Closes B2C "this isn't for me" objections by anchoring identity choice.
- **Phil Knight / Nike** *(anchor for "Resourcefulness Not Resources")*. Knight nearly bankrupt multiple times, got vendors to work for free, used resourcefulness when resources were zero.
