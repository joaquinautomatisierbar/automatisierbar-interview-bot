# Leads and Acquisition

> Frameworks for getting strangers to notice you, respond, schedule, and show up — across content, ads, outbound, and lead nurture.

**Sources distilled:** $100M Leads (Hormozi 2023), $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (Hormozi 2025), $100M Playbook: Hooks (Hormozi 2025), $100M Playbook: Marketing Machine (Hormozi 2025), $100M Playbook: GOATed Ads (Hormozi 2025), ACQ Advertising Handbook (Hormozi 2025 — 125pp, OCR-extracted).

## Frameworks

### The Core Four Advertising Methods — *Source: $100M Leads, Section III (master framework)*

There are only **four ways** to get engaged leads. Every advertising activity collapses into one of these:

| # | Method | Direction | Audience | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Warm Reach Outs** | 1-on-1 | People who know you | First Five Customers; nothing else; before you have an audience or money |
| **2** | **Post Free Content** | 1-to-many | People who know you | Building an audience; making warm reach-outs more effective; compounding |
| **3** | **Cold Reach Outs** | 1-on-1 | People who don't know you | When you have time + low budget; B2B; specific avatar lists |
| **4** | **Run Paid Ads** | 1-to-many | People who don't know you | When you have money + want speed/scale; mature funnels |

Every channel (email, IG, X, FB ads, podcasts, billboards, cold calls, sales reps, partners…) is a *flavor* of one of these four. There is no fifth way.

**Progression as the business matures:** Start at #1 (warm reach outs to your network — get the first 5 customers). Layer in #2 (free content) so reach outs become easier and warm leads compound. Add #3 (cold reach outs) when you can afford to scale outreach. Layer in #4 (paid ads) when you have enough margin and offer-fit to scale spend.

**Diagnostic:** "I tried advertising and it didn't work" = always means: ran one channel, on one platform, in one specific way, with a small audience. Almost never means: tried all 4 with sufficient volume.

### The Rule of 100 — *Source: $100M Leads, "More" chapter*

**100 primary actions per day, for 100 consecutive days.** Volume promise:

| Method | "Primary action" definition |
|---|---|
| Warm reach outs | 100 per day (email, text, DM, call) |
| Free content | 100 minutes/day creating + at least 1 release/day |
| Cold reach outs | 100 per day (email, text, DM, cold call, flyer) |
| Paid ads | 100 minutes/day creating ads + run them for 100 days straight |

If you can't do 100 of one, do 25 across 4. Volume is the floor. Quality optimization (the "Better" pillar below) only kicks in *after* you have data, which only comes *after* volume.

### More – Better – New (the scaling framework) — *Source: $100M Leads, "Get Lead Getters On Steroids"*

Three phases of scaling any of the Core Four:

| Phase | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| **More** | Crank up volume on what's already working. Double inputs → more leads even with no skill improvement. Apply Rule of 100. | First. Always. Most growth comes from here. |
| **Better** | Test ONE thing per week per platform at the biggest **constraint** (the step where most leads drop off). Log every test result. | After volume. Once CAC starts breaking from "More." |
| **New** | Open a new platform / new method / new audience. (Adding a Core Four method, new channel, new geography.) | When "Better" hits diminishing returns. Last, not first. |

**Constraint logic:** if your funnel is `30% optin → 5% apply → 50% schedule`, the "apply" step is the constraint (5% drop-off is where most are bleeding). A 5% lift on 5% (→10%) = **2× total leads**. Same 5% lift on 50% (→55%) = 1.1× leads. Find the constraint, test there.

**Anti-pattern: The Size of the Pie Fallacy.** Owners look at their niche on their platform and see 3-4 big competitors and assume the market is "split." The market is actually 100× bigger across other platforms, channels, audiences, and method-flavors. Most niches have room for unlimited new entrants who use a different Core Four flavor.

### Lead Magnets — 7-step construction — *Source: $100M Leads, "Engage Your Leads"*

A **lead magnet** is a *complete solution to a narrow problem* — free or low-cost — that creates a new problem your **core offer** solves. (The salty pretzels at the bar that drive drink sales.)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | Pick a narrow, meaningful problem. Make sure your core offer solves the *next* problem that surfaces after the lead magnet is consumed. |
| **2** | Pick the solution type: (a) **Reveal Their Problem** (diagnostic — speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection); (b) **Sample / Trial** (limited-use access to your core offer); (c) **One Step of a Multi-Step Process** (the first chapter / the first month / the first coat). |
| **3** | Pick the delivery vehicle: **Software** (calculator, spreadsheet, dashboard); **Information** (course, guide, video); **Service** (free audit, free first session); **Physical Product** (book, sample, kit). 3 types × 4 deliveries = up to 12 lead-magnet variants from one narrow problem. Make several, rotate. |
| **4** | Test the **name** (headline > image > subheadline). Same MAGIC formula as `offers-and-pricing.md`. Hormozi tested the *$100M Leads* book title via polls before printing — small word changes (adding "How to," removing "more") doubled engagement. |
| **5** | Make it easy to **consume**. Multi-format. Phone + computer. Image + video + text + audio. The $100M Offers book splits ¼/¼/¼/¼ across ebook/physical/audio/video — formatting alone 4×'d total reach. |
| **6** | Make it **darn good**. Goal: lead-magnet value > price of core offer. *Give away the secrets, sell the implementation.* Sucky-fluff lead magnets actively destroy your reputation; assume 99% won't buy but will judge you on the free version. |
| **7** | Make it **easy to ask for more** (the Call To Action). After consumption, prompt a clear next step that loops them into the core offer. |

**Critical rule:** *Grand Slam Offer principles apply to free stuff too.* Make your lead magnet so good people feel stupid not taking it. Free is not an excuse for low value.

### Hook – Retain – Reward (the content unit) — *Source: $100M Leads, "Post Free Content" chapter*

Every piece of audience-growing content has 3 components:

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Stop the scroll / grab the click. (Detailed treatment in the Hooks Playbook section above.) |
| **Retain** | Keep them watching/reading after the hook. Pacing, story, value density. Avoid promising more than you deliver. |
| **Reward** | Make consuming feel worth their time. Insight, laugh, status reinforcement, useful artifact. If they feel rewarded, they engage / share / come back. |

Posting free content does 3 things at once: grows the warm audience, makes warm reach-outs more effective (they can find you), and makes ALL other Core Four methods convert better (people see content → trust ↑ → lead magnets convert + ads convert + cold reach-outs convert).

**Volume rule:** "I went from posting once a week to ten times a week. My audience grew 10× as fast." Volume works.

### Four Pillars of Lead Nurture — *Source: Hormozi, $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture*

**What it is:** the four operational drivers of show rate, derived from millions of appointment-data rows analyzed by Vlad's ML team.

**Components:**
1. **Availability** — appointment slots × hours × days. The biggest single lever (an "almost perfect correlation" with throughput).
2. **Speed** — speed-to-first-contact, speed-to-first-appointment, speed-of-response.
3. **Personalization** — channel preference, qualification, best-leads-to-best-closers, segmented messaging, incentives, matched proof.
4. **Volume** — number of reach-outs before giving up; number of reminders; book-meeting-from-meeting (BAMFAM).

**When to use:** any business where leads need to schedule and show up before they can buy. Cold-call outreach, demo bookings, consultative sales, brick-and-mortar appointment businesses.

**Worked example (compressed):** Joaquin's Cold-Call pipeline lives entirely inside Pillars 2 and 4. Workflow A (transcript-analyzer) is downstream of show; Workflow C (audio→transcript) is downstream of show. The pipeline currently has zero infrastructure for Pillars 1 (availability) or 3 (personalization at outreach time) — both are unbuilt levers.

### 70-20-10 Innovation Rule (applied to hooks) — *Source: Hormozi, $100M Playbook: Hooks (citing Page/Brin)*

**What it is:** resource allocation for creative output — 70% to what's already proven, 20% to "winner-adjacent" experiments, 10% to wild new ideas.

**Components:**
- **70% Core** — re-use top-performing hooks from your own past content; rotate them into shorts, captions, ad openings.
- **20% Emerging** — model hooks that are working in adjacent niches; permutations of your own winners.
- **10% Big new ideas** — totally untested formats and concepts; document the losers, promote winners into the 70%.

**When to use:** any creative output flywheel where you have data (ads, social posts, emails, cold outreach openers, video thumbnails). Wrong move on day one is "10/30/60 — let's mostly try new things." Right move is "mostly do more of what works, fund a small portfolio of bets."

**Worked example (compressed):** Joaquin's LinkedIn Engagement Bot generates 3 voice-shaped variants per post (Erfahrung / Sicht / Frage). Today the variant chosen is logged in Notion but no feedback loop drives next-batch variant weighting. A 70-20-10 application would re-rank the prompt's variant strategies based on past chosen-variant frequency (e.g. if Frage gets picked 60% of the time, push more Frage-style prompts into the 70% bucket).

### Two-Part Hook Anatomy — *Source: Hormozi, $100M Playbook: Hooks*

**What it is:** every effective hook contains exactly two parts.

**Components:**
1. **Call-out** — gets the prospect to think *"this is for me"* (the cocktail-party effect: a single signal pierces noise).
2. **Condition for value** — implicit or explicit promise: *"if you keep watching/reading, you get X."*

**Verbal hook formats:**
- **Labels** — "Local business owners, I have a gift for you"
- **Questions (yes / open)** — "Would you pay $1000 for the business of your dreams in 30 days?"
- **Conditionals** — "If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong sh*t"
- **Commands** — "Read this if you're tired of being broke"
- **Statements** — "The smartest thing you can do today…"
- **Lists / Steps** — "28 ways to stay poor"
- **Narratives** — "One day this old lady comes in and she was piss angry…"
- **Provocative exclamations** — "Ahhhhh… this is the blueprint to becoming a millionaire…"

**Nonverbal hooks:** visual disruption (catching a banana mid-frame), audio signals, framing.

**When to use:** any first 3 seconds of any content. *"If nobody notices your ad, nothing else matters."*

### Awareness Pyramid + Ad Assembly Process — *Source: GOATed Ads Playbook (adapted from Eugene Schwartz)*

**Why ads hit a wall.** Most ad campaigns top out not because the market is saturated but because the *creative is.* The better the ad, the colder the audience it converts.

**The 5 Awareness Levels** — from warmest/smallest to coldest/largest. Each level needs a different hook archetype:

| Level | Audience | Hook archetype | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Most Aware** | Knows you, knows the product — just needs the deal | Offer-driven | "50% off our best-selling X this month" |
| **2. Product-Aware** | Knows your product but unsure it's right for them | Proof-driven | "Why 10,000 people chose X to solve Y last month" |
| **3. Solution-Aware** | Knows the problem + that solutions exist, doesn't know yours | Promise-driven | "The fastest way to [result] — introducing X" |
| **4. Problem-Aware** | Knows they have a problem, no idea about solutions | Pain-driven | "Tired of [problem]? There's a better way" |
| **5. Completely Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | Curiosity-driven | "The hidden danger in your daily routine costing you [money/time/health]" |

**Diagnostic for scaling:** if 90% of your hooks are level-1 (offer-driven), you're capped at the smallest audience. To unlock scale, *broaden the awareness levels you target.* When stuck, "go a little broader" — you keep the warm audience and pick up colder audiences.

### The Ad Assembly Line — Hook + Meat + CTA — *Source: GOATed Ads Playbook*

Don't make whole ads. Make ad *parts* in batches, then assemble.

**Time allocation in prep:**

| Component | % of attention | Output per session |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 80% | 50 hooks |
| **Meat** | 20% | 3-5 variants |
| **CTA** | ~0% | 1-3 variants |

**Math: 50 × 4 × 2 = 400 ads from one session.** From 5/month to 600/month. That's how Hormozi doubled a portfolio company in 2 quarters after they hit a "saturated market" wall at $200K/mo ad spend.

**Hook sources** (in priority order):
1. Your previous winning ad hooks
2. Hooks that worked in your free content (different platform, same opener)
3. Hooks from other people's high-performing ads (save them — phone album works)
4. Hooks from other people's high-performing free content
5. Platform ad libraries (last resort — hard to know which ads actually performed)

**The 5 Ad Meat Formats:**

| Format | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Demonstration** | Live use, unboxing, before/after, high-production hero (Dollar Shave Club, Old Spice) |
| **Testimonial** | UGC, podcast clips, raw iPhone, walk-n-talk rant, parade-of-proof group, lifecycle ads, man-on-the-street, influencer collabs |
| **Education** | Explainer videos, listicles, whiteboard tutorials, repurposed organic content |
| **Story** | Storytelling, lifestyle, warning, documentary, skits, brand manifestos |
| **Faceless** | Comment/text screenshots, text-only, slideshows, animations, cartoons, VFX |

**CTA components** (Clear > Clever):
- **What** to do (click button / call number / reply YES)
- **How** to do it (the literal mechanism)
- **When** to do it (urgency/scarcity attached)
- **What they get** for doing it (the immediate reward)
- **What happens next** *(optional but high-leverage for lead magnets / multi-step)*
- **Demonstrate** the next step visually — show the page they land on, the form, the submission. *Almost no one does this.*

CTA testing: pick 3 minor variants on the same hook+meat. Once you find a working one, stop testing CTAs — diminishing returns.

### The Ad Kaleidoscope — Multiplying Winning Ads — *Source: ACQ Advertising Handbook*

The natural extension of GOATed Ads. Once you have a winner, the Kaleidoscope gives you 50-100 variations of that winner — all of which tend to perform like the original. **80% of ad effort goes here**, 20% goes into trying genuinely new ads.

**The thesis:** Pareto applied to creative. 80% of ad results come from 20% of creative. So spend 80% of effort making MORE of what already works, not new untested swings.

**The 4-step process:**

| Step | Action | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify Winners** — your top 20% (or top 10% / top 5%) of past ads | One-time |
| 2 | **Remix Winners** — post-production tweaks to existing footage. Fast. Fatigues fast. | 80% of total |
| 3 | **Remake Winners** — re-record with the same script, new real-world variables. Slower. Lasts longer (months/years per winner). | (split with #2) |
| 4 | **Try New Ideas** — totally untested creative concepts. When one wins, it enters the Kaleidoscope. | 20% of total |

**The Henry Ford insight:** "New" doesn't mean "from zero." New means *new to the viewer.* Customers have a far lower threshold for "new" than the creator does. Same script, different filter = perceived as a new ad.

#### Remix variations (post-production, no re-recording)

Order easiest → hardest:

| Variation | What changes |
|---|---|
| **New Speed** | Run at 1.1x or 1.2x without distorting voice |
| **New Filter** | Black & white, sepia, contrast adjustment |
| **New Background / Border** | Color swap or thin border addition |
| **New Fonts / Captions** | Swap text styling in editor |
| **New Headline** | Replace text overlay with different copy |
| **New Medium** | Export winning video frame as a static image ad |
| **New Format** | Resize for each platform's native placement (square, vertical, story) |
| **New Meat (same Hook)** | Splice a different recorded body onto the winning hook |
| **New Visual Effects** | Stickers, motion graphics, animations — focus on the first ~3 sec (hook) |

#### Remake variations (require re-recording)

Order easiest → hardest:

| Variation | What changes |
|---|---|
| **New Clone** | Same script, hit record again. Fingerprint differences (lighting, sound, time) make it convert as new. |
| **New Props** | Add, swap, or remove props/costumes/accessories. (Removing the banana prop in Hormozi's "I caught a banana" ad created a new winner.) |
| **New Examples** | Same script, swap the names/numbers/case study. ("$4,664/mo last on leaderboard" → swap person + number, same script.) |
| **New Setting** | Same script, different location (beach / car / office / studio). |
| **New Talent** | Same script, different person — match talent to your customer demographic. **Wildly underutilized.** |
| **New Combination** | Stack multiple changes: new prop + new filter + new background. Endless permutations. |

**Cadence:** when you find a winner, immediately put it through the Kaleidoscope. Make 50-100 variations across remix + remake categories. Run them all. Find sub-winners. Put those through the Kaleidoscope again.

**Source library:** ACQ Advertising Handbook also contains 20 named ad frameworks (5 each from Gym Launch / ALAN / Skool / $100M Leads book launch). When designing a campaign from scratch, consult the source for the framework templates — they're shot-by-shot scripts with creative direction.

### The Marketing Machine — *Source: $100M Marketing Machine Playbook*

A 9-component checklist for **harvesting customer-generated proof** continuously — without the founder having to "dance on camera." Rotates the business out of "founder's face" risk and into a system that produces fresh ad creative weekly. (Hormozi's claim: 80% of his top-50 Gym Launch ads had no Hormozi face — they were customer-generated. Without this system, the company was unsellable at the first private-equity meeting; with it, sold for $46.2M.)

| # | Component | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lifecycle ads** | Recordings of sales / onboarding / support / ascension calls. Splice "before" moment from sales call + "during" from support + "after" from milestone call → a full customer journey ad. |
| 2 | **Social Media Scrape** | Branded hashtag (`#gymlord`, `#yourbrand`). Set reminders to grab tagged posts/stories before they expire. Folder organize. |
| 3 | **In-Person Events** | Stage ads (selfie + crowd shot end of day 1), professional testimonials (separate room, two cameras, hit-list of customers, 6-point script), and "man in the street" rapid-fire compilations on 10 prepared questions. |
| 4 | **Communication Scrape** | Search support / account-rep chats by win keywords (`PIF`, `sales`, `revenue`, `closed`, `leads`, `show`). Screenshot. Folder. Reward reps for submissions. |
| 5 | **Community Scrape** | "Wins" post category in your community. Friday-prompt customers to post. Screen-record scrolling through them for video ads. |
| 6 | **Reviews Scrape** | Google / Yelp / FB / Trustpilot / Amazon / Glassdoor — sort by newest, screenshot, file. |
| 7 | **Bonus Unlocks** | Create a juicy training/SKU. Customers can buy it OR submit a video testimonial to unlock it free. Same effort, two assets (video + post). |
| 8 | **Award Unlocks** | Aspirational milestone (e.g. "$1M/year gym"). Trophy/sword/engraved item. To redeem: 4 forms of proof (community post, emailed video, unboxing video, stage moment at next event). |
| 9 | **Competitions** | Annual/bi-annual: best raw iPhone testimonial wins (judged by *actual ad performance over 30 days*). Massive influx of fresh creative in one week. |

**Implementation order (Hormozi's recommendation):**
1. **Competition** (biggest immediate influx)
2. Chat/Communication scrape (zero new effort, just systematize)
3. Community scrape (same)
4. Reviews scrape (same)
5. Social media scrape (same)
6. Bonus unlocks (small editing required to make the bonus)
7. Award unlocks (needs the trophy/award itself)
8. Events (most effort, but highest-value capture density)
9. Lifecycle ads (most editing-intensive, save for last)

**The 6-point testimonial script** (use across components 3, 7, 8, 9):
1. **Internal struggle** — what was rock-bottom before? What did you have to do that you hated?
2. **External struggle** — what were your objective metrics (revenue, weight, score)?
3. **Skepticism** — what were your main concerns about buying?
4. **Overcome** — what made you do it anyway?
5. **External victory** — what are your objective metrics now?
6. **Internal victory** — best moment since? What can you do now you couldn't before?

**Hook the testimonial.** First 3-5 sec of the video is the single most important line. Have customers record several different opening lines; pick the strongest in post.

**Camera + audio rules for events:** two camera teams (one event, one testimonial room), hire extra hotel rooms if no second event room available, TEST audio + video before kickoff (worst case is losing every testimonial from an entire event), pay the handler extra $50 for every off-list person they snag.

**When to use:** As soon as you have any customers with results worth showing. The compounding compounds early — the first competition alone often produces a year of ad creative.

### EAD-style Lead Triage (rebrand: garbage-man framing) — *Source: Hormozi, $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture*

**What it is:** rep-level lead allocation that maximizes throughput at a sales team scale.

**Components:**
1. **Score every lead** on a 1-5 or red/yellow/green system based on demographics + actions taken at opt-in.
2. **Best leads → best closers.** Don't waste your top 1% rep on cold yellows.
3. **"Yellows are the new gold"** — assign yellow-rated leads to a hungry junior rep ("garbage man" identity); they often outperform mature reps cherry-picking greens because they get more swings.
4. **Track three rates per rep:** show rate, close rate, lead-to-close ratio. Don't reward close rate alone — that incentivizes cherry-picking.

**When to use:** any sales team with multiple reps and uneven lead quality. Exact same logic applies to a single-rep operation: time-block "yellow leads" outside prime hours rather than abandoning them.

## Heuristics & rules of thumb

- **"If they don't show, they cannot buy."** Show rate is one of the most leveraged numbers in any business with appointments. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Contact within 60 seconds of opt-in for a 391% conversion lift.** Within an hour for 7x qualification rate vs. >1hr. *Source: Lead Nurture / Velocify / HBR stats.*
- **78% of customers buy from the company that responds first.** *Source: Lead Connect, cited by Hormozi.*
- **Cap appointments at 72 hours out (5 days max).** Closer-in-time = higher show rate. The longer the wait, the more ghost. Pull appointments forward to today whenever possible. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Double-dial.** First call gets blocked by carrier filtering on unknown numbers; second call often gets through. (Obey local laws.) *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Reach out 7+ times before giving up.** 44% of salespeople stop after attempt #1; the average is 1.3. Anyone who runs a 7-attempt cadence beats the field by default. *Source: InsideSales.com / RAIN, cited.*
- **Local area code calls have significantly higher pickup rates** than out-of-area. Use call-wrapping tech to dynamically rotate area code. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Open initial outreach on multiple channels (call, text, DM, email); continue conversation on whichever channel they answered first.** *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **iPhone blue messages get higher response rates than green/SMS.** Invest in real iPhones for outbound texting if the channel matters. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **BAMFAM — Book A Meeting From A Meeting.** Never end a call with "we'll circle back." Pull up the calendar in real time. *Source: Sharran Srivatsaa via Hormozi.*
- **Front-load reach-out attempts in the first 24-48 hours.** Each passing day cuts schedule probability sharply. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Spend 80% of advertising effort on the hook.** "After you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar." *Source: David Ogilvy via Hormozi.*
- **Record 10 hooks for every 1 piece of ad content.** A/B-style: cycle hooks over the same body. *Source: Hooks.*
- **Reciprocity-based push incentives work even when transparent.** "I sent you a $5 gift card so you're caffeinated for our meeting." Show rate goes up regardless of whether the prospect knows the mechanism. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **A/B pull incentives** ("face cream or hand lotion?", "morning or afternoon?") double as commitment devices — the choice presupposes attendance. *Source: Lead Nurture.*

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- **Stopping after the first attempt.** Industry baseline; do the opposite. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Letting leads schedule >72 hours out by default.** You'll book more appointments and ghost more of them — net loss. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Single-channel outreach.** Email-only or call-only loses people who happen to live on a different channel that day. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Generic templated follow-ups.** Worse than no follow-up because they actively brand you as a spam operation. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Assuming the prospect will book again later if you don't BAMFAM the next call.** They won't; their motivation peaks in the moment. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Treating all leads with equal effort.** Score them. Match best to best. Don't run a junior rep against a green-rated lead. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Tracking close rate alone.** Incentivizes cherry-picking. Track *lead-to-close* as the holistic metric. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Spending equal time on the hook and the body of an ad.** The hook is 80% of the leverage. Inverted resource allocation = wasted spend. *Source: Hooks.*
- **Innovation-only creative strategy (10/30/60 instead of 70/20/10).** Burning your entire creative budget on untested ideas guarantees volatility and starves the proven engine. *Source: Hooks via Page/Brin.*
- **"I can't be open more hours."** What people really mean: "it's not worth it for me right now." But the cost of the missed appointments often dwarfs the marginal staffing cost. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Sending the same hook on every platform.** Different platforms reward different hook formats; cross-test rather than cross-post identical openings. *Source: Hooks.*

## Worked examples / case studies

- **Hormozi's gym thank-you page A/B.** Original: "Thank you, you'll get a text shortly to book." Iteration: "Leila will reach out at this number." Final winner: photo of Leila + "Text Leila at this number with the time that works for you." Crushed prior versions because both men and women preferred to text a real person. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Dean Graziosi × Larry King infomercial.** Original opening: "Tonight on Larry King, I'm here with multi-bestseller Dean Graziosi…" — bombed. Re-recorded only the hook: "Have you ever in your adult life looked at yourself in the mirror and thought 'I should be further ahead by now?'" — sold millions of books. Same content, same offer, same price; only the first sentence changed. *Source: Hooks (anecdote from Dean Graziosi).*
- **Insurance company BAMFAM intervention.** Acquisition.com bought an insurance co; biggest constraint was prospect drop-off between call #1 and call #2. Single intervention: rep books call #2 *during* call #1 instead of "I'll follow up." Second-call show rate skyrocketed; sales lifted directly. *Source: Lead Nurture.*
- **Jacob the "garbage man".** 15-year-old gets a cold-call seat at Gym Launch. Older reps cherry-pick greens; Jacob takes everyone's yellow leads. Coined "yellows are the new gold." Finishes top salesman out of 26 reps in his last quarter. Promoted to Director of Sales at Acquisition.com. *Source: Lead Nurture.*

- **LinkedIn engagement bot:** the 70-20-10 rule is directly applicable. Today the bot generates 3 fixed-strategy variants per post. After ~30 days of Notion-logged "chosen variant" data, the bot's voice prompt should re-weight strategies via 70/20/10 instead of staying fixed at 33/33/33.
- **Hook quality on cold-call openers:** Workflow A classifies outcomes but doesn't tag the *opener used*. If we add an `Opener Hook` field on the Lead-DB and Nico iterates 5-10 openers per week using the 70-20-10 rule, the Weekly Call Report (Workflow B) can produce a hook leaderboard — directly actionable feedback into Pillar-3 personalization.
