## Frameworks

### The Value Equation — *Source: $100M Offers, ch. 6 (the master construct)*

**The formula:**

```
                 Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                 Time Delay     ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```

Four drivers — top two you increase, bottom two you decrease:

| # | Driver | Goal | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dream Outcome** | ↑ | "What will I make / achieve / experience?" |
| 2 | **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** | ↑ | "How will I know it's going to work for *me* specifically?" |
| 3 | **Time Delay** | ↓ | "How long will it take?" |
| 4 | **Effort & Sacrifice** | ↓ | "What is expected of me?" |

**Why it's a division, not addition:** if you can drive Time Delay and Effort & Sacrifice toward zero, value approaches infinity. Beginners obsess over Dream Outcome (easy claims). Pros obsess over Time Delay + Effort (hard, defensible). Apple beat phone competitors on effort, Amazon on time delay, Netflix on both. Spend disproportionate effort on the bottom of the equation.

**Perception is reality.** London Underground rider satisfaction was lifted more by a few-million-dollar dotted countdown map (decreased *perceived* time delay) than by faster trains (which would've cost billions of actual seconds). When stuck, ask: is there a *psychological* solution where the *logical* solution has already been tried?

**When to use:** Every offer construction or improvement decision. If sales feel hard, it's almost always because one of the four drivers is weak. Diagnose which.

### The Grand Slam Offer & Offer Construction Process — *Source: $100M Offers, chs. 8-10*

A **Grand Slam Offer** = an offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Differentiated, uncomparable, value-stacked, risk-reversed. Outcome: increased response rates, higher conversion, premium prices — simultaneously.

**The 5-step construction process:**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify the Dream Outcome.** Sell the vacation, not the plane flight. "Lose 20lbs in 6 weeks" ≠ "$99/mo bootcamp." |
| 2 | **List every problem** the prospect will face — in sequence, in absurd detail. Each problem maps to one of the 4 value drivers (Dream Outcome / Likelihood / Effort / Time). 16+ core problems with sub-problems = 32-64 obstacles. The more you find, the better. |
| 3 | **Convert each problem into a solution** by reversing it: "Cooking is hard" → "How anyone can cook healthy food in under 5 minutes." Don't fancy this — just enumerate. |
| 4 | **Brainstorm delivery vehicles** for each solution across these dimensions: 1-on-1 / small group / 1-to-many; DIY / DWY / DFY; in-person / phone / text / Zoom / chat; recorded / live; expirations; personalization tier. **The 10x/0.1x test:** "If they paid me 10× my price, what would I provide? If 0.1×, how would I make it more valuable anyway?" |
| 5 | **Trim & stack.** Drop high-cost / low-value solutions. Keep low-cost / high-value AND high-cost / high-value. Then stack them as a named bundle. Aim for items where the cost-to-deliver discrepancy is largest (typically 1-to-many leverage). |

**Critical rule from the eating-out-guide story:** *Solve every perceived problem.* Don't get romantic about how customers should solve it your way. One unaddressed objection can kill the sale. Add the eating-out guide to the meal plan; close the sale you would've lost.

**Mantra for sequencing:** *"Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction."* Generate demand first, prove the offer works, then optimize delivery costs. Don't optimize a business that has zero customers.

### The Starving Crowd (Market Selection Before Offer) — *Source: $100M Offers, ch. 4*

Even a Grand Slam Offer in a dead market loses. The four criteria of a starving crowd:

| Criterion | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Massive Pain** | They're already actively suffering and seeking solutions. |
| **2. Purchasing Power** | They can afford what you charge. |
| **3. Easy to Target** | They're reachable as a defined audience (not "everyone who breathes"). |
| **4. Growing** | The market is on a tailwind, not contracting. |

If a market scores poorly on these, no offer construction or pricing skill compensates. Pick the market FIRST.

### Enhancing the Offer: SUBGN — *Source: $100M Offers, chs. 11-16*

Five psychological levers stacked on top of the core offer. (Hormozi doesn't use the SUBGN acronym — I'm using it here as a memory hook for **S**carcity, **U**rgency, **B**onuses, **G**uarantees, **N**aming.)

#### Scarcity — supply is limited
- Limited # of seats / spots / units
- Limited # of bonuses
- Never available again
- Triggers: people want what's rare, want what others want

#### Urgency — time is limited
- Rolling cohorts (next start = X days)
- Rolling seasonal (this offer disappears Friday)
- Pricing change (price goes up Monday)
- Bonuses-based urgency (bonus expires in 24h)

#### Bonuses — make the offer feel "but wait, there's more"
- Stack distinct named items, each with its own dollar-value tag → total stack value should be 10×+ the price
- Reframe price objections by anchoring against stack value
- Each bonus should solve a *specific* objection or problem from your problems list
- **Scarcity + Bonuses combo:** "First 10 buyers get bonus #3" doubles both levers

#### Guarantees — reverse the risk

Four taxonomy types:

| Type | What | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | "No questions asked, full refund within X days" | Lower-ticket B2C; you have high margin headroom; conversion-rate gain >> refund-rate gain |
| **Conditional** | "Get X result in Y time IF you do A, B, C — else Z" | Higher-ticket; ties refund to client behavior so you control outcome quality |
| **Anti-Guarantee** | "All sales final" + a strong reason why | High-cost-of-fulfillment products; consumables; transparency about your exposure |
| **Implied** | Performance-based (revshare, profit-share, ratchets, "I only get paid if you make money") | Trust + measurement available; aligns incentives |

**Guarantee math:** A 30% close rate lift with a 2× refund rate (5%→10%) is still a 23% net sales gain. Don't be emotional, do the math. (Jason Fladlien claimed 2-4× conversion lifts from improved guarantees alone.)

**Hormozi's favorite:** **Service Guarantee** — "We keep working free until you achieve X." Eliminates the time element. Almost no one redeems it because by the time they could, they're either close to the goal or feeling positive about you. Add condition: must do the actions linked to success.

**Pro tip:** *Name* your guarantee something cool. "30-day satisfaction guarantee" → "Club a Baby Seal Guarantee: after 30 days, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't pay a penny." Makes it sticky and quotable.

**Stack guarantees:** Combine an unconditional 30-day with a conditional 90-day. Stacks risk-reversal layers.

#### Naming — the MAGIC formula

| Letter | Stands for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | **Magnetic Reason Why** | "Free," "88% Off," "Spring," "Grand Opening," "New Year," "Anniversary" |
| **A** | **Announce Avatar** | "Local business owners," "Retired athletes," "Lakeway Moms," "Salon owners" — go hyper-specific (sub-market, not city) |
| **G** | **Goal** | "Pain Free," "Celebrity Smile," "First Client," "Six Pack," "Double Your Profit," "$10K" |
| **I** | **Interval** (time) | "21-day," "6-week," "12-week," "3-month" — skip if claim is income/health (platform compliance) |
| **C** | **Container** word | "Challenge," "Blueprint," "Bootcamp," "Intensive," "Incubator," "Masterclass," "Sprint," "Catapult," "System," "Shortcut," "Reset" |

Use 3-5 of MAGIC, not all 5 (length matters). Doesn't need to be in MAGIC order. **Bonus:** rhyme or alliterate where natural ("Six-Pack Fast Track," "Big Booty Bootcamp," "Make Money Masterclass," "Marriage Thrive Deep Dive").

**Examples in the wild:**
- Free Six-Week Lean-By-Halloween Challenge
- 88% Off 12-Week Bikini Blueprint
- 5 Clients in 5 Days Blueprint
- Lakeway Moms — $1,500 Off Your Kids' Braces

**Offer fatigue order** (when ads stop converting, change in this priority):
1. Creative (images/videos)
2. Body copy
3. Headline (the wrapper / MAGIC name)
4. Duration of offer
5. Free/discount enhancer
6. Monetization structure (last resort)

Most fatigue is solved at 1-3. Don't rebuild the engine when the paint is faded.

### Money Models — Sequence of 4 Offer Types — *Source: $100M Money Models, 2025*

A **Money Model** is a *sequence* of offers, not a single offer. The 4 building-block offer types:

| # | Offer Type | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Attraction Offers** | Turn strangers into customers (free / discounted / steep-deal entry). |
| **2** | **Upsell Offers** | Get customers who said yes to spend more cash (now or later). |
| **3** | **Downsell Offers** | Convert "no" into "yes" with a smaller / cheaper / lower-quantity version. |
| **4** | **Continuity Offers** | Recurring offers that keep cash flowing month after month. |

**Stacking rule:** Use all 4 in your Money Model. Attraction alone = lots of customers, no profit. Attraction + Upsell = some profit. Attraction + Upsell + Downsell = catches the nos. + Continuity = compounding monthly base.

**Five favorite Attraction Offers:** Win-Your-Money-Back, Giveaways (with partial-scholarship phone follow-up), Decoy Offer, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later.

**Win-Your-Money-Back (Hormozi's #1 Attraction Offer — generated $1B+ industry-wide):**
- Customer pays upfront. If they hit a goal OR complete actions OR both → they win the money back (cash or store credit).
- Three trigger types:
  - *Results-only:* hit weight-loss / revenue / metric goal
  - *Actions-only:* attend all sessions, post weekly progress, journal daily, complete homework
  - *Actions + Results:* both required (best for skill-development products)
- Criteria must be (a) easy to track, (b) likely to produce real results, (c) advertise the business (post in social, refer, leave reviews)
- **The play actually makes more money from those who succeed**, not those who fail. Apply store credit toward a longer/bigger upsell instead of refunding cash. ~10% take refund in well-run programs.
- **Don't take blood money:** if someone wants out, refund cleanly. Headache > pennies.
- **Make everyone a winner privately:** halfway through, tell them the credit applies to the long-term goal regardless of short-term outcome. Lowers anxiety, raises retention.

**Money Model anti-patterns:**
- Hard selling weak products. If someone doesn't want it, find someone who does.
- Lying / non-transparent offers. "Reputation can't file bankruptcy."
- Win-Your-Money-Back when refund rate is already >5%. Fix the product first.
- Cash refunds when store credit toward something better is available.

**When to use:** Most businesses have only one offer (the core sale). Adding even one more offer type doubles revenue per visitor. Adding all four is what scales.

### Customer-Financed Acquisition (CFA) — *Source: $100M Lost Chapters, Section B*

**Thesis:** "If you can spend more than your competition to get a customer, you'll get more customers than them." Master CFA = remove cash as the constraint on growth. Forever.

**The 3 levers:**

| Lever | Direction | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** (Cost to Acquire a Customer) | ↓ minimize | Total marketing + sales cost / new customers acquired. Includes ad spend, payroll, software, commissions. |
| **LTGP** (Lifetime Gross Profit) | ↑ maximize | Total gross profit a customer generates over their lifespan. NOT net profit. NOT revenue. |
| **PPD** (Payback Period) | ↓ minimize | Time until cumulative GP from a customer ≥ CAC. The faster, the more reinvestment cycles per year. |

**The math:**

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross Profit | Revenue − Cost of Fulfillment |
| Gross Margin | GP / Revenue, expressed as % |
| LTGP (transactional) | GP × avg # transactions per customer |
| LTGP (recurring) | GP / monthly churn rate |
| Churn | Customers lost in period / customers at start of period |
| CAC | All marketing + sales costs / customers acquired |
| Payback Period | Days/months until cumulative GP ≥ CAC |

**The 3 Levels of CFA:**

| Level | 30-Day Cash vs CAC | What it means | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** | 30D Cash < CAC | You eventually break even, but it takes >30 days. Floats on savings/loans/credit lines. Risky. | Outside capital |
| **Level 2** | 30D Cash = CAC | You break even within 30 days = within a credit-card cycle. Use 0%-interest credit, pay off monthly. Credit limit = ad budget cap. | Credit limit |
| **Level 3** | **30D Cash > 2× CAC** | Customer pays for *themselves AND another customer* within 30 days. Forced viral growth. **No outside capital ever needed.** | Operations / fulfillment, never cash |

**The doubling table** (Level 3 played out, reinvesting all extra profit):

| Month | New customers this month | Total customers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 6 | 32 | 63 |
| 12 | 2,048 | **4,095** |

That's *one initial $1 of cash* turning into 4,095 customers in 12 months. Hormozi's actual case studies in the book: software co $60K/mo → $1.7M/mo in 6 months. Consulting biz $215K/mo → $1.8M/mo in 8 months. E-commerce $80K/mo → $1.5M/mo in 6 months. **Same mechanism every time.**

**Why 30 days specifically:** any business can get interest-free credit for 30 days (credit cards). Pay off balance before due date → cost of capital is zero. After Level 3, the credit card stops mattering — your *previous* customers fund the *next* batch directly.

**Hormozi's "Most Prized Metric": 30D Cash.** The single number he tracks across portfolio companies. Maximize this and you've won the acquisition game.

### Your First Avatar — Vista's Method — *Source: $100M Lost Chapters, intro chapter*

The **#1 highest-leverage move** Hormozi runs in every Acquisition.com portfolio company. From the Vista Equity Partners playbook (~$50B in software exits): *score customers by value, double down on the channels that bring the high-scoring ones, kill the others.* Pareto on steroids.

**4-step process:**

1. **Survey existing customers.** Higher engagement if live (event/call) vs form. Reward completion. Categories:
   - **Demographics:** age, gender, location, marital, role
   - **Business stats** (if B2B): revenue, profit, employee count, churn, pricing, # customers, time in business
   - **Aspirations:** what was their goal when they bought? What problem were they solving?
   - **Buying process:** single biggest reason they bought, trigger event, content consumed before buying, # pieces consumed, when they first heard of you vs when they bought, source channel, referral source

2. **Sort by your favorites: highest spenders + longest tenure.** Top 20%. Ignore the bottom 80%.

3. **Find what they have in common.** Read all answers. Use your brain. *"I know. Thinking is hard. The good news is your competitors won't do it."* Goal: 3-5 qualifiers they all share.

4. **Execute (two parts):**
   - **Speak the new avatar.** Update ads, pages, messaging to address them directly. Repel the bad fits. Stop selling anyone who doesn't meet the requirements. *Stop. Selling. Them.*
   - **Reverse-engineer the buying process.** Look at what your best customers consumed before buying. Force every new lead through that same path.

**Hormozi's Gym Launch findings (after running the process):**
- *Demographics:* right-leaning, married, 25-45, male, US-based gym owner
- *Business floor:* signed lease, 1+ employees, $10K+/mo revenue, 30+ existing clients
- *Aspirations:* $1M+/yr gym, less hours, more locations
- *Buying triggers:* not enough leads, "bad market," bad pricing, can't find good employees
- *Buying behavior:* **78% of top customers consumed at least 2 long-form content pieces** before buying

**Action from buying-behavior finding:** every lead, before sales, gets pushed through 2 long-form content pieces. Sales team hand-picks 2-3 from the "all-time greatest hits" list. Force the buying journey that *worked.*

**The 70× profit gap.** Same vertical (fitness business services), same total sales count. Hormozi made **70× the profit** of the competitor. Not skill — *segmentation*. Competitor sold to anyone with a credit card → high churn, generic advice, low retention, low margin. Hormozi sold only to top-20% avatars → premium pricing, low churn, high retention, repeat business.

> *"You make more because of who they are, not because of who you are."*
> Same work, $1M/mo customer worth 100× more than $100K/mo customer (% lift × revenue). Charging 10% of growth: $40K/yr vs $4M/yr from same effort.

**Pro tip — when you have no customers yet:** start with the industry you know most about (friends, family, past jobs). Pick the segment you can help the most. Narrow target → serve them → re-run the avatar analysis once you have 20+ customers.

**Pro tip — sell to people who don't stop buying.** Some segments inherently buy forever (enterprise B2B, established practices, recurring-cost businesses). Some don't (one-time-purchase consumers, struggling startups). Pick the former.

### The Three Levers — *Source: Pricing Playbook, "Pricing to Make the Most Money"*

The genie story. You can double one lever and hold the others constant:

| Lever | Effect on profit (Hormozi's worked example) |
|---|---|
| 2× new customers (cuts CAC in half) | **3.5× profit** |
| 2× purchase frequency (cuts churn in half) | **3.5× profit** |
| **2× price** (same everything else) | **6× profit** |

**When to use:** When deciding where to invest growth effort. Pricing wins because it lifts revenue AND drops costs (fewer customers to serve at the new price). Independently corroborated by Profitwell's 512-company study: 1% pricing improvement is ~2× as profit-efficient as retention and ~4× as acquisition.

**Implication:** Default to "test pricing first" before pouring money into ads or retention plays.

### Three Pricing Models — *Source: Pricing Playbook, "Three Models of Pricing"*

| Model | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Simple, covers costs | Leaves money on the table; customers don't care what it costs you |
| Competitor-based | Simple, market-anchored | Copies someone else's business model; race to the bottom |
| **Value-based** | Charges what customers will pay; forces continuous value improvement | Requires customer conversation + product iteration |

**Use value-based.** The other two are diagnostic only ("am I above/below the floor?").

**Three metrics to find the value-based price:**
1. Conversion rate at price X
2. Churn at price X
3. Resulting LTV (gross profit over customer lifetime)

The "best price" = the one that maximizes the **Total Return** column when you tabulate price × conversion × LTV. Often counterintuitive (e.g. 2× price + 20% fewer conversions still wins).

### The Ten Instant-Profit Pricing Plays — *Source: Pricing Playbook*

Tactical plays Hormozi runs across his portfolio. Stacked, they total **+26.8% to +63.8% revenue uplift**. Pick the ones that fit; even one matters.

| # | Play | Lift | One-line how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly → 28-day billing** | +8.3% | 13 cycles/yr instead of 12. Display weekly, bill 4-weekly. |
| 2 | **Processing fee + 2nd payment form** | +3-4% revenue OR +14-51% LTV | Add 3.99% CC fee; offer to waive if customer adds 2nd card → kills involuntary churn (1.2-1.7%/mo recovered). |
| 3 | **Sales tax line item** | 0-10% | "Plus tax" on invoice; don't eat it. Or incorporate in tax-friendly state. |
| 4 | **Annual CPI increase** | 3-10%/yr compounding | Bake 5-15% annual price increase into contracts. 5%/yr → +22% over 4 years. |
| 5 | **Annual billing** | +5x LTV potential | Annual cycle drops churn from 10.7% to 2% monthly (Profitwell). Discount 16% to incentivize. Get 30% take if it's the default. |
| 6 | **Round up (.99 / 7→9)** | 4-11% | Change $47 → $49.99 etc. Skip for luxury (rounds to 0/5). |
| 7 | **Annual renewal fee on top of monthly** | +10-25% | Advertise low monthly + charge $99/yr "rate protection." People watch monthly price, ignore annual. |
| 8 | **Automatic continuity** | +10-30% LTV | After main service ends, auto-roll into 5-20% price "continued access" tier. Disclosed up front, not sneaky. |
| 9 | **Ultra-high anchor** | +10-15% | Add a 10×-priced "mac daddy" SKU. ~10% take it, the rest perceive your core offer as cheaper → close rate also goes up. |
| 10 | **Guarantee / warranty upsell** | +5-20% | After sale: "Want the standard warranty?" Price at 5-30%. ~1 in 20 redeems. Pure margin if priced ≥ replacement cost. |

### The RAISE Letter — *Source: Price Raise Playbook*

Five-section letter for announcing a price increase to existing customers:

| Letter | Section | What goes here |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | Remind them of value delivered | Personalize: features used, results delivered, time as a customer |
| **A** | Address the price change directly | One sentence. Rip the bandaid. "To continue investing in X, we need to raise prices." |
| **I** | Invest in their future | List specific things you'll do with the extra revenue (hiring, training, equipment, tech, facility, service level). Frame each as "more good stuff" or "less bad stuff" for them. |
| **S** | Soften with a loyalty discount | Give them a credit/extension that expires in 3-6 months. Reframes raise as "gift now, normal price later." |
| **E** | Explain away concerns + PS escape valve | "Reply directly to me." PS: "If this materially affects your ability to [buy groceries / run your business], let me know and we'll work something out." |

**When to use:** Any time you raise prices on existing customers. Works for both 10% bumps and 3× resets (Trey gym example: $99 → $299, kept 70% of customers, went from -$2K/mo to +$4K/mo profit in one week).

**Stair-step variant** for big raises: drop the discount in tranches every 3-6 months instead of one cliff. Easier for customers to swallow.

### Virtuous vs Vicious Price Cycle — *Source: Price Raise Playbook*

| ↓ Lower price triggers… | ↑ Higher price triggers… |
|---|---|
| Lower client emotional investment | Higher emotional investment |
| Lower perceived value | Higher perceived value |
| Lower client results (because of above) | Higher results |
| **Higher** client demandingness | **Lower** demandingness |
| Less service capacity (less margin) | More service capacity (more margin) |
| Lower team conviction in selling | Higher team conviction |
| Lower owner gratitude for customers | Higher gratitude |

**Implication:** Pricing isn't just a revenue lever — it changes the *kind of customer you attract* and the *kind of business you run*. Cheap = grumpy. Premium = loyal.

### Fast Cash Play (quarterly warm-list promo) — *Source: Fast Cash Playbook*

**What it is:** a limited-time, scarcity + urgency + premium-priced offer to your warmest audience (current customers, past customers, engaged leads). Designed to extract maximum cash in 7 days from people who already trust you.

**Audience:** existing + past customers + engaged leads only. Cold leads fatigue the play.

**Offer:** unscalable, premium, exclusive. Stack 1-3 core components + 3 bonuses. Drip the bonuses across the 7-day window — each pushes another tier of fence-sitters over the line. Justify the premium price with categories that don't scale: 1-on-1 attention, personalization, convenience (24/7), status, year-long duration, priority/speed, white-glove experience, in-person access, network intros, vetted-vendor secrets.

**Quantity cap:** 5-10% of customer base. Selling out fast makes the *next* play more compelling.

**Price:** 10x to 50x normal cart value. The price selects the buyer.

**Cadence:** every ~90 days. More frequent = list fatigue. Less frequent = wasted upside.

**The 10x-the-10% Rule:** if 10% of customers pay 10x the price, you double revenue — and because CAC is already paid, virtually all of it drops to profit.

**Two campaign sequences:**

| | Push to consult (B2C/B2B with sales calls) | Push to automated checkout (high-volume) |
|---|---|---|
| **Day -7** | Primer text: "Sending you something tomorrow" | — |
| **Day -6 / -5** | Announcement email + text. List 1-3 core components. Include price range. CTA = book a call. | T-5: primer. T-4: announcement + cart-opens-soon. |
| **Day -3** | Sales update: how many sold, how many left. Reminder. | T-2: cart opens in 48h. |
| **Day -2** | "X spots left" + bonus #2. | T-24h: bonus #1 + set-an-alarm. |
| **Day -1 (AM)** | Warning + bonus #3 (best one). | T-0: cart open + bonus #2. |
| **Day -1 (PM)** | Sold-out announcement. | +60min: sales update. +6h: bonus #3. +12h: sold out. |
| **Optional** | "Back-out one spot left" if someone drops. | Same. |

**ROI benchmark (Hormozi's gym example):** $25K/mo gym (166 members × $150) at 25% margins = $75K/yr profit. Add 4 quarterly Fast Cash Plays @ ~$57K each = $229K extra revenue/yr. Net income: $75K → $281K. ~4× profit lift from 4 email-and-text campaigns.

## Principles

- **Profit is unnatural — you must force it into existence.** *Source: Pricing Playbook epigraph.*
- **Price for maximum lifetime money, not max first-purchase price.** Most owners optimize the wrong horizon.
- **Hearing more "no"s ≠ less money.** Higher prices, lower close rate, more total revenue is the common pattern. *Why it works:* most markets have a long tail of higher willingness-to-pay than the median.
- **High close rate (>50%) = prices too low.** If almost everyone says yes, you have headroom. Same for full capacity.
- **Raising prices usually means raising value.** Bigger raise → bigger value upgrade required. Don't raise prices without something to point to.
- **Believe in your price.** If you don't believe your product is worth the new number, fix the product before the price.
- **Bill less frequently → lower churn.** Each billing event is a churn opportunity. Annual: 2%/mo churn. Monthly: 10.7%/mo churn (Profitwell).
- **Match billing cadence to value cadence.** Don't bundle one-time value (info, setup) with recurring value (accountability, ongoing access). Customers cancel when the lookback window inside their last billing cycle shows weak value.
- **Display price in smallest increment, bill on longest.** "$5/day, billed annually at $1,825" works better than "$1,825/year."
- **Customer surplus = goodwill.** The gap between price and perceived value is what drives word-of-mouth and renewals. Premium price + premium product = max surplus per customer.
- **Different customer avatars need different prices.** It's normal for one segment to have 5-10× the willingness-to-pay of another. Multi-tier pricing captures both — name the tiers aspirationally.

## Heuristics & rules of thumb

- **The 1% pricing rule (Profitwell, 512 companies):** 1% pricing improvement = 2× as profit-efficient as 1% retention improvement, 4× as 1% acquisition improvement. → If you have one hour to improve profit, spend it on pricing.
- **Test pricing on new customers first.** Validates the new number AND gives you confidence/social proof when you roll it to existing customers.
- **Raise prices at least once per year.** Warren Buffett raised See's Candies prices 50× in 51 years. Inflation alone justifies 3-5%/yr; product improvement justifies more.
- **Pair price raises with a launch (within 90 days).** Frames as "early adopter pricing" and rides launch momentum.
- **Meet 1-on-1 with each customer if the raise is >50%.** Volume permitting. Shopify-competitor case: CEO called all 300 customers, raised prices 100×, lost 1 customer.
- **Customer LTV % distribution after a raise:** ~1 month of elevated churn (people who were going to cancel anyway, pulled forward) → month 2 below baseline → month 3 returns to normal. Don't panic at the spike.
- **For .99 endings:** non-luxury yes; luxury no (luxury wants round 0/5 because round = no-bargain = status).
- **Ultra-high anchor pricing:** 10× core offer is the right multiple. ~10% will take it; the rest perceive core as cheaper.
- **Guarantee pricing:** set the guarantee fee ≥ your cost of fulfilling a replacement. Then the play is mathematically free profit even at high redemption rates.
- **"Plus tax" works.** Restaurants do it. SaaS does it (Canadian marketer charging $25K + 8% sales tax = $27K). Stop eating other people's costs.
- **Fast Cash Plays make ~15% of revenue but ~50% of profit** in the businesses Hormozi has run them in. *Source: Fast Cash Playbook.*
- **Cap your Fast Cash inventory and announce sell-out.** Confirms scarcity wasn't theatrical. *Source: Fast Cash.*
- **Save the strongest Fast Cash bonus for the last 24 hours.** That's when fence-sitters convert. *Source: Fast Cash.*
- **"Revenue is vanity. Profit is sanity. Cash is reality."** *Source: Fast Cash Playbook epigraph.*

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- **Grandfathering existing customers when you raise prices.** Locks you into legacy pricing forever. Trey kept his $49/mo customers when he should've raised them; the cheap customers were also the most demanding.
- **"Lifetime access" for a one-time fee.** Theoretically defensible, practically a death spiral. No large company does this. *Why:* forever lasts longer than the money.
- **Setting price by looking at competitors' prices.** Most of them are broke. Copying broke people gets you broke. (Dan Kennedy: "There's no strategic advantage to being the second-lowest price in a marketplace. But there is for being the highest.")
- **Not raising prices because you're afraid of complaints.** Trey was 12 weeks from closing when he tripled prices. Fear of complaints → guaranteed business death.
- **Adding expenses just to justify a price raise.** Negates the entire benefit. The "investments" in the I-section of the RAISE letter should be things you were going to do anyway.
- **Locking in a price on a long contract when you can avoid it.** Future-you will want to deliver more (and charge more). Don't tie your hands.
- **Bundling info products + accountability into one price.** Info gets underpriced (one-time-value), accountability gets overpriced (ongoing-value). Split them.

## Worked examples / case studies

- **Trey's gym (Price Raise Playbook):** $49/mo memberships, customers were the worst, business losing $2K/mo. Tripled prices to $147 via the gym price-increase letter (6-page version). Lost some members, retained majority. Profit went to +$4K/mo within a week. Same business, same product, only price changed.
- **Hormozi gym personal example (Pricing Playbook rules):** $99 → $299 (3×). Lost 30% of customers. Revenue went from 200 × $99 = $19,800 to 140 × $299 = $41,860 (>2× revenue), AND delivery costs dropped 30%. Profit lift was multiples larger than revenue lift.
- **Restaurant + 4% processing fee:** $1M/yr revenue, 9% margins → $90K profit. Add 4% processing fee → $40K straight to bottom line → $130K profit (44% lift) for the cost of reprinting menus.
- **Tanning chain annual renewal fee (John's mentor):** Advertise $39/mo → real annual is $39×12 + $99 renewal = $567 → effective $47/mo (+20%) without affecting conversion.
- **Workshop speaker's continued access:** $750K/yr in pure profit from one contract line. After 1-yr course access ends, auto-rolls into $99/mo continuity. Sales unaffected because customers know upfront and know they can cancel.
- **Hormozi physical-products portfolio company (warranty upsell):** Existing 10-yr warranty, started selling it at 10% of product price. Massive opt-in rate. Almost no one redeemed → near-pure profit, recouped half the sales team's commissions.
