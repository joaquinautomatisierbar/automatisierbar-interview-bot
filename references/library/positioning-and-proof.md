## Frameworks

### Branding = Deliberate Pairing — *Source: Branding Playbook*

**What it is:** branding is the act of pairing your business (logo, name, founder, product) with things your ideal audience already likes. Done well, the audience transfers their existing positive associations onto your brand. Done poorly, they transfer negative associations.

**The cattle metaphor:** original meaning of "brand" is the sear on a cow. A no-brand cow is anonymous. A branded cow that you recognize triggers behavior — you bring it home if it's your neighbor's, you eat it for a month if you hate the neighbor. **Same cow, different behavior, all from the brand.**

**Three states:**
- **Good branding** = paired with stuff a *majority* of your ideal audience likes.
- **Bad branding** = paired with stuff a *majority* dislikes.
- **No branding** = invisible; exposure produces no behavior change.

**Why it matters in numbers:** Hormozi's worked example contrasting branded vs unbranded for the same physical product:

| | Price | COGS | Gross profit/unit | $2K ad spend → CTR | Conversions | Sales | Total profit | RoAS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unbranded | $2,500 | $500 | $2,000 | 1% (100 clicks) | 4% (2 sales) | 2 | $4,000 | 4:1 |
| Branded | $5,500 | $500 | $5,000 | 3% (300 clicks) | 6% (18 sales) | 18 | $90,000 | **45:1** |

A strong brand simultaneously: lifts CTR (more people click), lifts close rate (better leads), and unlocks premium pricing — then compounds over future purchases. Same ad spend, **11× the return**.

**When to use:** any business that wants to escape the commodity trap. Direct response is for those who can't brand. Branding is for those who want lasting pricing power.

### Reach × Influence × Direction — *Source: Branding Playbook*

A brand isn't a single quality. It has three orthogonal measurements:

| Axis | Definition | Bad → Good |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | How many people know about you | "Nobody" → "Everyone" |
| **Influence** | When they see you, does behavior change? | "They glance and forget" → "They act immediately" |
| **Direction** | Towards (buy/support) or away (avoid)? | Away → Towards |

**Eight brand positions** result. Examples (Hormozi's perspective):

| Reach | Influence | Direction | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | Towards | Elon Musk (he buys Teslas + Starlink) |
| High | High | Away | Terrorists (everyone knows, everyone repels) |
| High | Low | Towards | Taylor Swift (likes her, won't buy much) |
| Low | High | Towards | Close friends |
| Low | High | Away | Copycats / scammers |
| Low | Low | Towards | Acquaintances |
| Low | Low | Away | Strangers in the street |

**Implication for operators:** if your ad spend isn't producing sales, isolate which axis is failing. Are people not seeing it (reach)? Seeing and ignoring (influence)? Or seeing and turning away (direction)? Different fixes.

### Branding ≠ Advertising — *Source: Branding Playbook*

| | Definition | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| **Advertising** | Letting people know your stuff exists | Reach |
| **Branding** | What you make them associate with you | Influence + Direction |

You can have great advertising and bad branding (Pepsi × Kendall Jenner protest ad). You can have great branding and bad advertising (every great-but-undiscovered product). The combinations are independent.

**Settles "any publicity is good publicity":** No. *"Any publicity is better than no publicity, but good publicity is better than bad."* Bad publicity beats invisibility but loses to good publicity.

### 4-Step Brand Build/Pivot Process — *Source: Branding Playbook*

| Step | Action | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pick an ideal customer avatar** | Growing market, has money, easy to target, in pain. All four. |
| 2 | **Figure out what they like** | List 5-10 things this avatar already values, struggles with, aspires to. |
| 3 | **Associate your brand with those things** | Make content / ads / appearances / partnerships that pair you with those liked things. |
| 4 | **Optimize associations for max profit** | Track which pairings produce purchases; double down on the productive ones. |

The build is slow. Branded ads outperform unbranded ads at multi-x ratios — but it takes time to seed the pairings. *"Speaking from experience, branded returns on advertising are absurd. They just take time. And that's why no one does it."*

### Three Levels of Authority — *Source: Branding Playbook*

The strongest associations come from sources the audience already trusts. Three escalating levels:

| Level | Source | Authority weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What you say** about yourself (content, ads, outreach) | Lowest — every business says they're great |
| 2 | **What other people say** about you (affiliates, testimonials, reviews) | Medium-high |
| 3 | **What customers experience themselves** (their own results) | Highest — drives word-of-mouth, retention, premium-price tolerance |

**Operator implication:** every dollar spent moving up a level outperforms a dollar spent doubling down on the level below. A new ad campaign (Level 1) loses to a single great testimonial video (Level 2), which loses to a customer's own result that they share unprompted (Level 3).

### Vista's Avatar Method — Top-20% Customer Analysis — *Source: $100M Lost Chapters, intro*

The single highest-leverage move Hormozi runs in every Acquisition.com portfolio company. Adapted from how Vista Equity Partners (~$50B in software exits) values acquisitions: score customers, double down on channels that deliver the high-scorers, kill the rest. **Pareto on steroids.**

**4-step process:**

1. **Survey existing customers.** Live (event/call) > form for engagement. Reward completion. Question categories:
   - Demographics (age, gender, location, role, marital, etc.)
   - Business stats if B2B (revenue, profit, # employees, churn, pricing, # customers, time in business)
   - Aspirations ("What was your goal when you bought? What problem were you solving?")
   - Buying process ("Single biggest reason you bought? Trigger event? Content consumed before buying? How many pieces? When did you first hear of us vs when you bought? Source channel? Referrer?")

2. **Sort by your favorites:** highest spenders + longest tenure. **Top 20%.** Ignore the bottom 80%.

3. **Find common factors.** Read all answers. Use your brain. *"I know. Thinking is hard. The good news is your competitors won't do it."* 3-5 qualifiers all top customers share.

4. **Execute (two parts):**
   - **Speak the new avatar:** rewrite ads, pages, messaging. Repel bad fits. **Stop selling anyone who doesn't qualify.** Pour effort into the channels that deliver these customers.
   - **Reverse-engineer the buying journey:** what did your top customers consume before purchasing? Force every new lead through the same path.

**Hormozi's Gym Launch findings (after running the process):**
- *Demographics:* right-leaning, married, 25-45, male, US-based gym owner
- *Business floor:* signed lease, 1+ employees, $10K+/mo revenue, 30+ existing clients
- *Aspirations:* $1M+/yr gym, fewer hours, more locations
- *Triggers:* not enough leads, "bad market," bad pricing, can't find good employees
- *Buying behavior:* **78% of top customers consumed at least 2 long-form content pieces** before buying

→ Action: every lead gets pushed through 2 long-form pieces before sales call. Sales reps hand-pick from "all-time greatest hits" content list. Force the journey that *worked.*

**The 70× profit gap (case study).** Same vertical (fitness business services), same total sales count. Hormozi made **70× the profit** of a competitor. Not skill — *segmentation*. Competitor sold to anyone with a credit card → high churn, generic advice, low retention. Hormozi sold only to top-20% avatars → premium pricing, low churn, repeat business.

> *"You make more because of who they are, not because of who you are."*

**Pro tip — when you have no customers yet:** start with the industry you know most about (friends, family, past jobs). Pick the segment you can help most. Narrow target → serve them → re-run avatar analysis once you have 20+ customers.

**Pro tip — sell to people who don't stop buying.** Some segments inherently buy forever (enterprise B2B, established practices, recurring-cost businesses). Some don't (one-time consumers, struggling startups). Pick the former.

**Re-run cadence:** every 6-12 months. Customer base evolves; the top-20% avatar drifts.

### The 13-Item Proof Checklist — *Source: Proof Checklist Playbook*

**Definition:** *Proof = data that supports your advertising claims.* The same testimonial can land as an atomic bomb of persuasion or as a meaningless footnote depending on how it's captured and presented. 13 dimensions move a proof point along the **belief continuum** (100% doubt ↔ 100% certainty).

| # | Dimension | Less compelling → More compelling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Setting** | Virtual → **In-person** (live demonstration in the prospect's hand beats screen video) |
| 2 | **Format** | Recorded → **Live** (live testimonial in the room beats a slick edit) |
| 3 | **Production** | Processed → **Raw** (iPhone unedited beats studio-edited; the rougher the more authentic) |
| 4 | **Modality** | Tell → **Show** (video of result beats verbal description) |
| 5 | **Source** | You saying it → **Other people saying it** (you're the most biased person about your product) |
| 6 | **Demographic match** | Opposite of avatar → **Identical to avatar** (use proof from people who look/sound/feel like the prospect) |
| 7 | **Specificity of pain** | Generic → **Personal** ("I was fat then skinny" loses to "I couldn't wear what I liked because my thighs would chafe and my marriage became sexless") |
| 8 | **Magnitude** | No result → Small result → **Big result** |
| 9 | **Recency** | Old → **New** (always be capturing — old proof signals you haven't had a result lately) |
| 10 | **Volume** | Less → **More** (floor-to-ceiling reviews; quantity compensates for any single weak point) |
| 11 | **Verification** | Unverified → **Third-party verified** (Amazon reviews, BBB, accreditation, awards, certifications) |
| 12 | **Numbers** | Without numbers → **With numbers** (specific stats with collection methodology beat anecdote) |
| 13 | **Language** | Technical jargon → **Metaphors** (relate the unknown to something the audience already understands) |

**Stacking rule:** the more checkboxes a single proof point hits, the heavier its persuasive weight. **Best possible proof** = in-person, live, raw, show-not-tell, third-party endorsed, recent, demographically matched, specific, big result, with verifiable numbers, expressed via metaphor. Almost no single proof hits all 13 — collect a portfolio.

### Match Your Claims To Your Proof (not the other way around) — *Source: Proof Checklist*

**Anti-pattern:** start with a bold claim, then hunt for proof to back it up.
**Pattern:** start with the proof you actually have, then make claims that the data already supports.

If your proof isn't compelling enough for the claim you want to make, **fix reality** — give services away free, work with better customers, improve the product — until the proof is real. *"Your promise is not a differentiator. Your proof is."* Anyone can copy your promise. No one can copy your data.

---

### Belief Continuum — *Source: Proof Checklist*

Belief isn't binary. It's a sliding scale from **100% doubt** to **100% certainty**. Each piece of proof moves the prospect along that line, never to either extreme. Marketing/sales' job is to push them as far towards "certainty" as possible, then ask for the sale at exactly the right moment.

**Operator implication:** stop measuring marketing as "did the customer convert." Start measuring "how far did this proof move the median prospect on the belief continuum?" That reframes content, ads, and outreach into a portfolio of incremental belief-shifters.

## Heuristics & rules of thumb

- **"You only get one name. Invest accordingly."** *— Warren Buffett, via Branding.*
- **"It takes 20 years to build a reputation and 5 minutes to ruin it."** *— Buffett, via Branding.*
- **Pricing power is the #1 indicator of business quality.** *"If you have to have a prayer session before raising the price by a tenth of a cent, you've got a terrible business."* — Buffett. Brand strength → pricing power. *Source: Branding.*
- **You can be widely positive without being polarizing.** Taylor Swift, Mother Teresa, Apple — strong brands with high reach + high influence + towards-direction without controversy. Polarization is *one* path, not the only one. *Source: Branding.*
- **Showing-not-telling beats every other proof tactic per dollar.** Capture before/after video; capture the moment of result. Whatever the customer is feeling at that breakthrough moment, it can never be reproduced after the fact. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Print every 5-star review you get and frame it floor-to-ceiling in your lobby.** Local-business proof tactic. Hard to disbelieve hundreds of testimonials at eye level. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Have a digital "proof page" with hundreds or thousands of testimonials.** Hormozi reports prospects who stayed up all night reading and converted. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Use blue iMessage, not green SMS, for personal-touch reach-outs.** Real-human signal. *Source: Lead Nurture (cross-pollinated).*
- **Capture the milestone moments (first weigh-in, first sale, first acceptance letter)** — those are the most emotionally charged proof captures, and they only happen once. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **For B2C avatars: feature people who literally look like your customers in your ads.** When Gym Launch ran female founder ads, they got more female founder leads. Same for older. The avatar in the ad becomes the audience in the funnel. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Specificity of pain > generic pain.** "We were two months from shutting our doors" hits ten times harder than "Business was tough." Coach customers to be specific in interview prompts. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Pair every technical mechanism with a metaphor.** SEO is to ads what an investment account is to a paycheck — slow start, compounding payoff. Customers buy what they understand. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Pick an avatar that is growing + in pain + has money + is easy to target.** All four. Drop one and the brand-build gets harder. *Source: Branding.*
- **Reach × Influence × Direction is independent — diagnose the failing axis.** A brand failing at "direction" (people see + react negatively) needs a different fix than one failing at "reach" (no one sees). *Source: Branding.*

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- **Starting with the brand "feel" (logo, colors, fonts).** That's not what makes brands work. Pairings make brands work. Hormozi spent 6+ years thinking branding meant aesthetics; nothing changed until he started making *deliberate associations*. *Source: Branding.*
- **Copying competitor positioning.** They're broke. *"There's no strategic advantage to being the second-lowest price." — Dan Kennedy.* *Source: cross-ref Pricing.*
- **Promising bigger and bigger results to out-promise competitors.** Anyone can copy your claim. They cannot copy your proof. Compete on proof, not promises. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Making testimonials too polished.** Heavy editing, music, post-production = lower conversion than the raw iPhone clip. *Source: Proof Checklist (winning Gym Launch ad analysis).*
- **Center-staging yourself in your own ads.** 80% of Hormozi's winning Gym Launch ads had no Hormozi, no education, no offer focus — just pure customer proof. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Letting "any publicity is good publicity" drive your decisions.** Bad publicity is better than no publicity, but good publicity is better than bad. Don't deliberately court bad publicity. *Source: Branding.*
- **Trying to be everything to everyone.** Narrow avatar = stronger pairings = sharper brand. Business law firm targeting "everyone" loses to one targeting assisted-living facility owners. *Source: Branding.*
- **Pivoting brand without pivoting content.** Hormozi noticed his audience was drifting from operators → wantrepreneurs because his content had drifted. To pivot brand, you have to pivot the *associations* — not just the logo. *Source: Branding.*
- **Old testimonials sitting on the homepage with no recent activity.** Implies you haven't had a recent result. Recency check: every testimonial dated. Refresh quarterly minimum. *Source: Proof Checklist.*
- **Treating testimonial collection as a one-time project.** Build it into the customer journey: prompt at milestones, incentivize raw video submission, automate the ingestion. *Source: Proof Checklist.*

## Worked examples / case studies

- **Hormozi's supplement business friend** *(Branding)*. Friend's audience would have netted +$500K/yr if they switched to Hormozi's supplements (better products, 4× the commission). Friend wouldn't switch. Why? The other supplement brand was *cool*. Hormozi's wasn't. He learned: budget for product + commissions ≠ budget for brand. The "cool factor" is paid pairing work, not luck.
- **Pepsi × Kendall Jenner** *(Branding — bad pairing)*. Ad pulled within 6 days. Good advertising (millions saw it) + bad branding (millions hated the protest pairing). Sales fell. Lesson: protests + Pepsi + Kendall Jenner = wrong pairing for *this* audience. The same Jenner could have done a different ad, different angle, that would have worked.
- **Gym Launch's "top 50 ads of all time" diagnosis** *(Proof Checklist — origin story)*. Ad costs doubled, fund manager called Hormozi in for lunch. He sorted thousands of historical ads by performance, bucketed them by theme, found the winning pattern: 80% of winners were *pure proof*, no education, no offer-focus, no Hormozi-on-camera. Just customer results.
- **Cale's gym story testimonial** *(Proof Checklist)*. One customer's testimonial — full gym to outsourced sale within 18 months for $250K — converted more than any other Gym Launch ad ever made. Big result + identical demographic + show-not-tell + recent + specific + numbers. Multiple proof boxes hit at once.
- **Floor-to-ceiling testimonial wall** *(Proof Checklist)*. Hormozi's recommendation: print all your 5-star reviews, frame them cheap, line your lobby. Massive lift in walk-in conversions. Volume of proof > quality of any single proof when the format allows.
- **Assisted-living law-firm avatar exercise** *(Branding step 1-3)*. Hormozi's worked example for narrowing an avatar. Generic "business law firm" → "law firm for assisted-living facility owners" → content topics list (depreciation tactics, recruiting talent, lawsuits, taxes) → content production → audience build. The narrowing is what makes the brand build possible.
