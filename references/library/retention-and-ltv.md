# Retention & LTV

> Decisions about onboarding, churn reduction, customer journey, lifecycle plays, lifetime value math, and getting customers to spend more over time.

**Sources distilled (so far):** $100M Retention Playbook (Hormozi, 2025), $100M Lifetime Value Playbook (Hormozi, 2025).

---

## Frameworks

### Lifetime Value (LTV) — *Source: LTV Playbook*

**Definition:** LTV = gross profit over the lifespan of a customer. Same as LTGP (Lifetime Gross Profit) or CLV.

**Why it's the master metric:**
> "He who can make a customer more valuable than his competition, wins."

The cost of getting a customer can only fall to zero. LTV can scale infinitely. Whoever has the highest LTV can outbid every competitor on every paid channel — a *monopoly of attention*.

**Calculation (3 steps):**

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| 1. Gross profit per transaction | Revenue − Delivery cost. (NOT net profit — this is pre-overhead.) |
| 2. Average transactions per customer | Either average from CRM data, OR — for recurring — `1 / churn` |
| 3. LTV | Transactional: `Gross Profit × # transactions`. Recurring: `Gross Profit / Churn` |

**Worked example (services):** $3,000/mo × 80% gross margin = $2,400 GP/customer. At 5% monthly churn → $2,400 / 0.05 = **$48,000 LTV.**

### The Crazy 8: Eight Ways to Increase LTV — *Source: LTV Playbook*

| # | Lever | One-line how |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Raise prices** | Affects gross profit more than any other lever. 10% margin biz + 20% price raise = 3× profit if sales hold. |
| 2 | **Decrease delivery cost** | Higher staff:client ratios, offshore talent, productize delivery, DFY → DWY, cap usage, lifetime → annual, in-person → remote, kill meetings, prepay vendors for 10-20% off. |
| 3 | **Increase # of purchases** | Three sub-tactics: (a) add recurring, (b) decrease churn, (c) follow-up campaigns to reactivate. |
| 4 | **Cross-sell something different** | Add a complementary product. 20% of customers buying a $100 add-on = +$20 LTV. |
| 5 | **Upsell quantity** | Three flavors: bulk (12× prepay), more frequent (1×/mo → every 3 wks), bigger (1 hr → 3 hr per session). |
| 6 | **Upsell quality** | Premium version of the same thing. Faster response, more access, better materials, 1-on-1 vs group, custom vs templated, DFY vs DIY, in-person vs remote, longer expiration, no-cancellation-fee, etc. |
| 7 | **Downsell quantity** | For unqualified-for-main-offer prospects only. "Smaller burger." Beats walking away with $0. |
| 8 | **Downsell quality** | Same. Slower response, group call, recorded vs live, junior staff, longer wait. Captures revenue from prospects who would otherwise leave. |

**Hormozi's favorite upsell of all time** *(empirically validated against the founders' preferred direction in the LTV playbook's opening case study — 2.2× LTV per customer)*:

> *"More of — or more help with — what they just bought… but with faster results, less risk, less effort, less hassle — for more money."*

**Critical rule on downsells:** Forbid sales reps from selling a *qualified* prospect a downsell. Downsells are for prospects who don't qualify for the main offer. Otherwise you cannibalize your premium SKU.

### Five Horsemen of Retention — *Source: Retention Playbook (Gym Launch era)*

Discovered by interviewing every gym owner with <3% monthly churn. The five common factors:

| # | Horseman | The play |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Track attendance** | When attendance drops from 3 → 2 sessions/wk, that's the early-warning signal. Reach out *then*, not at cancellation. |
| 2 | **Reach out 2×/week** | Praise participation + progress. Solve small problems. (Same Acknowledge-Complement-Ask pattern as the cancel-save script.) |
| 3 | **Handwritten cards** | At signup, at 3/6/12-month milestones, at birthdays/holidays. Hand-written, not printed. |
| 4 | **Member events** | Cadence: every 21, 42, or 63 days. Regular to you, random to them. Reduces churn AND drives referrals. |
| 5 | **Exit interviews** | Set the expectation at onboarding ("we require an exit interview"). Saves ~50% of cancellations that show up. |

**Result curve when rolling out the 5 Horsemen:**
- **Month 1:** churn UP ~50% ("shaking the three" — people who churned consumer-side but were still being auto-billed cancel + people who would've left anyway)
- **Month 2:** churn DOWN ~50% (retention gains kick in)
- **Month 3:** churn DOWN another ~50%

Net: 9% → 3% churn = **3.3× LTV.** Brace for the month-1 spike.

### The Churn Checklist (9 levers, in order) — *Source: Retention Playbook (modern Skool-platform data)*

Each lever stacks. Hormozi: *"I have never not made more money by going through and applying this checklist."*

| # | Lever | Core action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Find activation points** | Of churned customers who stayed >3 months and spent the most: what did they DO? That's the activation point. (Skool: 3+ posts in community. Gym Launch: invest recouped in first 30 days. Software: first dashboard login.) |
| 2 | **Onboard to the activation point** | Custom > Generic. Personal > Group. Live > Recorded. Carrots > Sticks. **Some > None.** Resell the purchase. Tell them how to unlock more value. Schedule next touchpoint. |
| 3 | **Incentivize activation** | Unlockables: courses, 1-on-1 calls, event tickets, badges, lifetime free at high engagement levels. Time unlocks just *past* major churn points. |
| 4 | **Community linking** | "Easy to quit a membership, hard to leave a relationship." Manually intro members. Group events. Community podcast. Elevate micro-celebrities. |
| 5 | **Correct or fire bad customers** | 3-strike rule on bad posters. Pin best posts daily. Reward good behavior publicly. Topic categories: Wins / Fun / Discovered / Meetups. |
| 6 | **Add annual pricing** | "Buy 10 get 2 free" = 16% off. Get 10-20% take rate via website checkout, 30%+ if it's the default option. Big-head/long-tail variant: $6,800 setup + $199/mo recurring. |
| 7 | **Cancellation calls or videos** | Save ~50% of those who hop on. Two saves: redo OR upsell to higher program. "Let me give it another shot." Or low-volume alternative: cancellation video reminding them why they joined + what they lose. |
| 8 | **Survey 2× per year** | "If I removed everything but one, what stays?" + "If I kept everything but removed one, which?" Reveals the core 2-3 things customers actually value. |
| 9 | **4-step customer journey** | **Activate → Testimonial → Refer → Ascend.** Steps 2-4 happen in any order, but plan for all four. Ascension is critical: customers will buy more — from you or someone else. |

### The A-C-A Reach-Out Framework — *Source: Retention Playbook*

For every customer touchpoint between onboarding and renewal:

- **A**cknowledge: name something specific they did
- **C**omplement: praise the specific behavior or result
- **A**sk: a question that invites engagement

Cadence: every 2-3 weeks for premium customers. *Counterintuitive finding:* the wealthier the customer, the LESS handholding they want.

### Price / Value / Churn relationship — *Source: Retention Playbook + LTV Playbook (cross-cuts)*

Higher price → higher churn (usually). But pricing decisions need to optimize the **Total Return** column, not just churn:

| Price | Conv | Churn | LTV | Total Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | 5% | 10%/mo | $100 | $500 |
| $20 | 4% | 10%/mo | $200 | $800 ← best |
| $100 | 2% | 33%/mo | $300 | $600 |

**Implication:** lowest churn ≠ best business. Price for max total LTV across the funnel.

## Principles

- **Existing customers > strangers.** They already trust you, paid you, gave you their card, are your ideal avatar, and have zero acquisition cost. Invest effort there *first*.
- **5% retention improvement = 25-95% profit increase.** *Source: HBR study cited in Retention Playbook.* The smallest churn improvements compound dramatically.
- **5-25× cheaper to retain than acquire.** *Source: industry studies cited.* Retention spend should be at least 1/5 of CAC.
- **Referrals > Churn = compounding growth.** If 10% churn but 10% referral rate, you're flat. Move either lever and you grow forever.
- **Less but better, not more and decent.** "Overwhelm is the #1 reason for churn." Hormozi's friend's $500K/mo newsletter had lowest churn with ONE Q&A call/month + ONE physical newsletter. Adding more services *increased* churn.
- **Value per second, not seconds of value.** Density beats duration.
- **Make consumption easy.** Most people don't finish the YouTube video, the course, the meal plan. Reduce effort and sacrifice. When customers stop consuming → that's your retention alarm.
- **Match billing model to value model.** Don't bill recurring for one-time-value content (info, setup). Bill one-time for one-time, recurring for recurring. (Same principle as offers-and-pricing.)
- **"Who" beats "what."** Serving better customers is the highest-leverage retention move. 10% improvement on a $100M business = same work as 10% on $1M = 100× the leverage. Find them, price for them, ignore the rest.
- **Activation point > everything.** Find what your stickiest customers DO, then onboard everyone to do that. All other levers compound this one.
- **Retention work feels obvious. Almost no one does it.** "Because it's work." That's the whole moat.

## Heuristics & rules of thumb

- **Activation funnel:** export churned customers → filter to those who stayed >3 months → take top 20% by spend → find common factors. Those are your candidate activation points.
- **Onboarding hierarchy:** custom > personal > live > carrots. But *some* always beats *none*. Group onboarding beats no onboarding.
- **Personalized 1-on-1 onboarding** beat group onboarding by **+25% ascension rate** in one of Hormozi's portfolio companies — was the largest contributor to scaling from $2M/mo to $2M/wk.
- **Time unlocks past churn points.** If most leave at month 3 → put a juicy unlockable at month 4.
- **Price annual at "buy 10 get 2 free" (16% off)** for default-via-website. If selling phone/webinar, can go steeper for higher take rates.
- **Cancel-save script:** Get more upset than they are. "That's completely ridiculous. You're 100% correct. Will you give me the opportunity to make it right?" Then: "If these things happened, you'd be happy?" → that's your roadmap.
- **For low-volume / high-price:** cancellation calls always pay off (you learn AND save).
- **For high-volume / low-price:** use a cancellation video instead. Resell why they started + what they lose.
- **B2B add-on rule:** the cross-sell that wins is the one closest to what they already bought. NOT the one you're passionate about.
- **Usage churn = leading indicator.** Annual contract but they stopped using at month 6 → high churn risk. Re-engagement now beats waiting for renewal.
- **Structural churn is real and OK.** If you serve VSMBs (very small businesses), they go out of business. That's not your problem to solve — Shopify and Skool both built huge co's at relatively high churn rates.

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- **Selling something you're passionate about instead of what customers want.** Opening case study of LTV playbook: founders wanted product they loved. Hormozi insisted on customer survey. Survey said the *adjacent* upsell. Implemented that → 2.2× LTV → 5× ad spend. *"The business serves the customer, not the other way around."*
- **Adding more services hoping it raises retention.** Often it raises overwhelm → raises churn. Less is more.
- **Selling downsells to qualified prospects.** Forbid this. Downsells are for the unqualified pool only — otherwise they cannibalize your premium offer.
- **One-and-done products in a recurring-revenue model.** Mismatch between what customers value (one-time) and what you bill them (recurring) = churn.
- **Skipping cancel-save calls because they don't scale.** Even at high volume, cancel-save is among the highest-ROI minutes of your week. The *learning* alone justifies it.
- **Treating onboarding as documentation.** Onboarding is a sales conversation that resells the purchase + drives behavior. PDFs alone don't onboard.
- **Lifetime access as a one-time payment.** Forever is longer than the money. (See `offers-and-pricing.md` for the full anti-pattern treatment.)
- **Optimizing churn alone instead of LTV.** Lowest churn ≠ best business. The $100 product with 33% churn might be worse than the $20 with 10% — but it's not always. Run the math.
- **Generic mass-audience advertising for a recurring business.** Hormozi vs his Gym Launch competitor: same CAC, but his customers were $42K LTV vs the competitor's $5K — because he targeted only customers with the longest expected stay. **70× more profit on identical CAC.**

## Worked examples / case studies

- **Gym Launch turning point (Retention Playbook).** 1,000 gym-owner customers, 15% monthly churn = 83% lost in a year. Hormozi DOD-consulting style: interviewed every gym with <3% churn, found the 5 common factors → 5 Horsemen. Rolled out, watched churn spike (month 1) then crater (months 2-3). Saved the customers, saved his customers' businesses, saved his business.
- **Skool platform (Retention Playbook, modern era).** Hosts who get 3+ group members stick way longer. So Skool built tools to *help* hosts get the first 3 members. Activation point engineering, not just measurement.
- **Founder-product survey case (LTV Playbook opening).** Acquired company. Founders wanted upsell A (passion). Hormozi wanted upsell B (adjacent to existing product). Asked customers — overwhelming preference for B. Implemented B. **2.2× LTV → 5× ad spend → revenue from a few million/month to a few million/week.**
- **Newsletter friend (Retention Playbook).** $500K/mo newsletter. Lowest churn was at his MOST minimal product (1 Q&A/mo + 1 physical newsletter). Every addition increased churn. *"Sometimes less is more. There is wisdom in deletion."*
- **Group→1-on-1 onboarding shift (Retention Playbook).** Portfolio company moved from group onboarding to 1-on-1. **+25% ascension rate** → largest contributing factor to scaling $2M/mo to $2M/wk.
- **Hormozi vs gym competitor (Retention Playbook).** Same ad spend, same CAC. Customers worth $5K vs $42K LTV. **70× profit gap from targeting alone.** "Common factors" analysis on top 20% of his own best customers.
- **Gym Launch 8% → 3% churn (Retention Playbook).** Identified activation point: customers who recouped investment in first 30 days stayed longest. Built "Fast Cash Play" to drive every new client to recoup quickly. **Churn halved in 6 months.** *(See also `offers-and-pricing.md` for the Fast Cash Play distillation.)*
